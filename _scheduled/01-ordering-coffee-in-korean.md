The easiest natural way to order coffee in Korean is to name the drink and add **주세요** (*juseyo*), “please give me.” For example, **아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** (*amerikano han jan juseyo*) means “One Americano, please.” You do not need a long sentence, but you do need to recognize the questions that normally follow: hot or iced, which size, for here or to go, and how you will pay.

A cafe conversation also has a practical order. You choose the drink first, answer customization questions next, say whether you are staying or leaving, and then pay. The mini-dialogues below follow that real counter sequence.

## The Core Pattern for Ordering

The most reusable formula is:

**[drink] + 한 잔 주세요** — *[drink] + han jan juseyo* — “One [drink], please.”

**한 잔** (*han jan*) is the counter for one cup or glass. It is optional, so **라떼 주세요** (*ratte juseyo*) is also correct. Adding it simply makes your quantity clear.

Useful first orders include:

- **아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** (*amerikano han jan juseyo*) — “One Americano, please.”
- **카페라테 한 잔 주세요** (*kaperate han jan juseyo*) — “One cafe latte, please.”
- **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*) — “This one, please.” Point to the menu when the name is difficult.
- **뭐가 맛있어요?** (*mwoga masisseoyo?*) — “What is good here?”

Some menus write 카페라떼, but the standard spelling is **카페라테**. In everyday speech, simply saying **라테** (*rate*) or the widely seen menu form 라떼 will be understood.

## Follow the Real Cafe Conversation

At many counters, the barista greets you and waits for your order. After you name the drink, they may ask about temperature, size, or where you will drink it. They would not normally ask what kind of coffee you want after you have already specified a latte.

Here is a realistic basic exchange:

You: **안녕하세요. 아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo.* — “Hello. One iced Americano, please.”  
Them: **어떤 사이즈로 드릴까요?** — *eotteon saijeuro deurilkkayo?* — “Which size would you like?”  
You: **작은 사이즈로 주세요.** — *jageun saijeuro juseyo.* — “A small size, please.”

If you have not said hot or iced, you may hear:

You: **카페라테 한 잔 주세요.** — *kaperate han jan juseyo.* — “One cafe latte, please.”  
Them: **따뜻한 걸로 드릴까요, 아이스로 드릴까요?** — *ttatteuthan geollo deurilkkayo, aiseuro deurilkkayo?* — “Would you like it hot or iced?”  
You: **따뜻한 걸로 주세요.** — *ttatteuthan geollo juseyo.* — “Hot, please.”

In **드릴까요?** (*deurilkkayo?*), the barista is politely asking what they should give or prepare for you. You can answer with the option plus **-로 주세요** (*-ro juseyo*), “please make it/give it as that option.”

## Customize Without Building a Complicated Sentence

Short requests are easier for both you and the barista. Add one change at a time:

- **샷 추가해 주세요** (*syat chugahae juseyo*) — “Please add an extra shot.”
- **시럽 빼 주세요** (*sireop ppae juseyo*) — “Please leave out the syrup.”
- **얼음 조금만 넣어 주세요** (*eoreum jogeumman neoeo juseyo*) — “Please put in only a little ice.”
- **우유 대신 두유로 바꿔 주세요** (*uyu daesin duyuro bakkwo juseyo*) — “Please change the milk to soy milk.”

Availability and extra charges vary by cafe, so ask rather than assume a substitution is possible:

You: **두유로 바꿀 수 있어요?** — *duyuro bakkul su isseoyo?* — “Can you change it to soy milk?”  
Them: **네, 가능해요.** — *ne, ganeunghaeyo.* — “Yes, that is possible.”  
You: **그럼 두유로 바꿔 주세요.** — *geureom duyuro bakkwo juseyo.* — “Then please change it to soy milk.”

For an allergy, do not rely on a vague preference phrase. Name the ingredient and ask directly: **우유가 들어가요?** (*uyuga deureogayo?*) means “Does it contain milk?” Staff can then check the recipe or product information.

## Say “For Here” or “To Go”

The barista may ask **드시고 가세요?** (*deusigo gaseyo?*), literally “Will you have it and then go?” In context, it means “For here?” A common paired question is **드시고 가세요, 가지고 가세요?** (*deusigo gaseyo, gajigo gaseyo?*), “For here or to go?”

Answer with either:

- **먹고 갈게요** (*meokgo galgeyo*) — “I’ll have it here.”
- **가지고 갈게요** (*gajigo galgeyo*) — “I’ll take it to go.”
- **테이크아웃 할게요** (*teikeuaut halgeyo*) — “I’ll get it to go.”

Here is the choice in context:

You: **바닐라라테 한 잔 주세요.** — *banillarate han jan juseyo.* — “One vanilla latte, please.”  
Them: **드시고 가세요, 가지고 가세요?** — *deusigo gaseyo, gajigo gaseyo?* — “For here or to go?”  
You: **가지고 갈게요.** — *gajigo galgeyo.* — “I’ll take it to go.”

## Pay, Get the Receipt, and Find Your Drink

Once the order is confirmed, the barista states the total. You can usually present your card without saying anything, but these phrases are useful:

- **카드로 할게요** (*kadeuro halgeyo*) — “I’ll pay by card.”
- **현금으로 할게요** (*hyeongeumeuro halgeyo*) — “I’ll pay in cash.”
- **영수증 주세요** (*yeongsujeung juseyo*) — “The receipt, please.”
- **영수증은 괜찮아요** (*yeongsujeungeun gwaenchanayo*) — “I don’t need the receipt.”

A staff member may give you a pager or call your order number. **진동벨이 울리면 가져가세요** (*jindongberi ullimyeon gajyeogaseyo*) means “Please pick it up when the pager buzzes.” If you are unsure where to wait, ask **어디에서 기다리면 돼요?** (*eodieseo gidarimyeon dwaeyo?*), “Where should I wait?”

## A Seven-Step Cafe Script

1. Greet the barista: **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*), “Hello.”
2. Order the drink: **아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** (*amerikano han jan juseyo*).
3. State the temperature: **아이스로 주세요** (*aiseuro juseyo*), “Iced, please.”
4. Choose the size: **중간 사이즈로 주세요** (*junggan saijeuro juseyo*), “A medium size, please.”
5. Say where you will drink it: **가지고 갈게요** (*gajigo galgeyo*), “To go.”
6. Pay: **카드로 할게요** (*kadeuro halgeyo*), “I’ll pay by card.”
7. Close politely: **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), “Thank you.”

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I order a latte in Korean?

Say **라테 한 잔 주세요** (*rate han jan juseyo*), “One latte, please.” You may also see 라떼 on menus; both will be understood in a cafe.

### How do I say that my coffee is to go?

Say **가지고 갈게요** (*gajigo galgeyo*), “I’ll take it to go.” **테이크아웃 할게요** (*teikeuaut halgeyo*) is another common and clear option.

### How do I ask for a cafe recommendation?

Ask **뭐가 맛있어요?** (*mwoga masisseoyo?*), literally “What is delicious?” It naturally means “What is good here?”

### What if I do not understand the barista?

Say **죄송하지만, 다시 말씀해 주세요** (*joesonghajiman, dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Sorry, but please say that again.” To ask them to slow down, use **천천히 말씀해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Please speak slowly.”

## Practice Before You Reach the Counter

The goal is not to memorize every possible cafe question. Practice the sequence: drink, option, for here or to go, and payment. Once those four turns feel automatic, an unfamiliar menu becomes much less intimidating.
