When buying clothes in Korea, a few compact phrases will carry you from the rack to the register. Use **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*) for “How much is this?”, **입어 봐도 돼요?** (*ibeo bwado dwaeyo?*) for “Can I try it on?”, and **다른 사이즈 있어요?** (*dareun saijeu isseoyo?*) for “Do you have another size?”

The language is straightforward, but store policies vary. Fitting rooms may have item limits, sale merchandise may have different exchange rules, and displayed prices are generally not invitations to bargain. Read signs and ask for information instead of assuming that every clothing shop works the same way.

## Browse and Ask for Help Naturally

Staff may greet you or ask **찾으시는 거 있으세요?** (*chajeusineun geo isseuseyo?*), “Are you looking for something?” If you are only browsing, answer:

**그냥 구경하고 있어요** — *geunyang gugyeonghago isseoyo* — “I’m just browsing.”

If you want help, name the item:

- **티셔츠 찾고 있어요** (*tisyeocheu chatgo isseoyo*) — “I’m looking for a T-shirt.”
- **청바지 있어요?** (*cheongbaji isseoyo?*) — “Do you have jeans?”
- **이거 보여 주세요** (*igeo boyeo juseyo*) — “Please show me this.”

You: **안녕하세요. 티셔츠 찾고 있어요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. tisyeocheu chatgo isseoyo.* — “Hello. I’m looking for a T-shirt.”  
Them: **어떤 색깔 찾으세요?** — *eotteon saekkkal chajeuseyo?* — “What color are you looking for?”  
You: **검은색 찾고 있어요.** — *geomeunsaek chatgo isseoyo.* — “I’m looking for black.”

The staff’s **찾으세요?** (*chajeuseyo?*) is an honorific way to ask what you are seeking. Your answer can use the simpler polite form **찾고 있어요**.

## Find the Right Size and Color

Korean brands do not all use identical sizing. Some labels use letters, some use numbers, and the fit can differ by brand and item. Ask for the specific option rather than assuming a familiar label will fit.

- **다른 사이즈 있어요?** (*dareun saijeu isseoyo?*) — “Do you have another size?”
- **더 큰 사이즈 있어요?** (*deo keun saijeu isseoyo?*) — “Do you have a larger size?”
- **더 작은 사이즈 있어요?** (*deo jageun saijeu isseoyo?*) — “Do you have a smaller size?”
- **다른 색깔도 있어요?** (*dareun saekkkaldo isseoyo?*) — “Do you have it in another color too?”
- **검은색으로 보여 주세요** (*geomeunsaeg-euro boyeo juseyo*) — “Please show me the black one.”

You: **더 큰 사이즈 있어요?** — *deo keun saijeu isseoyo?* — “Do you have a larger size?”  
Them: **네, 이 사이즈 입어 보세요.** — *ne, i saijeu ibeo boseyo.* — “Yes, try this size.”  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — “Thank you.”

When pointing, **이거** (*igeo*) means “this,” **그거** (*geugeo*) means “that” near the listener, and **저거** (*jeogeo*) means “that over there.” These words let you communicate even when you do not know the item’s Korean name.

## Ask Before Using the Fitting Room

Use **입어 봐도 돼요?** (*ibeo bwado dwaeyo?*), “Can I try it on?” The phrase literally asks whether trying it on is allowed. Staff may point to the fitting room, tell you an item cannot be tried on, or explain how many pieces you can take inside.

You: **이거 입어 봐도 돼요?** — *igeo ibeo bwado dwaeyo?* — “Can I try this on?”  
Them: **네, 피팅룸은 저쪽에 있어요.** — *ne, pitingnumeun jeojjoge isseoyo.* — “Yes, the fitting room is over there.”  
You: **네, 알겠습니다.** — *ne, algetseumnida.* — “Okay, I understand.”

After trying it on, describe the fit:

- **잘 맞아요** (*jal majayo*) — “It fits well.”
- **조금 커요** (*jogeum keoyo*) — “It is a little big.”
- **조금 작아요** (*jogeum jagayo*) — “It is a little small.”
- **저한테 안 맞아요** (*jeohante an majayo*) — “It does not fit me.”

## Understand Prices and Promotions

Ask **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*), “How much is this?”, if the tag is missing or unclear. To confirm whether a marked promotion applies, use:

- **이거 할인 상품이에요?** (*igeo harin sangpumieyo?*) — “Is this a discounted item?”
- **표시된 가격이 할인가예요?** (*pyosidoen gagyeogi haringayeyo?*) — “Is the displayed price the sale price?”
- **행사는 언제까지예요?** (*haengsaneun eonjekkajiyeyo?*) — “Until when is the promotion?”

Do not treat discount requests as standard in every Korean clothing store. Department stores, brand shops, shopping malls, chain stores, and many boutiques use fixed posted prices. Some market sellers may also use fixed prices. Ask about a discount only when a sign advertises one or the seller explicitly indicates flexibility. In that situation, **할인 가능해요?** (*harin ganeunghaeyo?*) means “Is a discount possible?” It is a question, not a claim that you deserve a lower price.

You: **표시된 가격이 할인가예요?** — *pyosidoen gagyeogi haringayeyo?* — “Is the displayed price the sale price?”  
Them: **네, 이미 할인된 가격이에요.** — *ne, imi harindoen gagyeogieyo.* — “Yes, it is already the discounted price.”  
You: **알겠습니다. 이걸로 주세요.** — *algetseumnida. igeollo juseyo.* — “I understand. I’ll take this one.”

This exchange is more useful than assuming that a market location, cash payment, or polite request automatically creates a discount.

## Pay and Check Exchange Conditions

At the register, say **이걸로 주세요** (*igeollo juseyo*), “I’ll take this one.” Then choose your payment method:

- **카드로 할게요** (*kadeuro halgeyo*) — “I’ll pay by card.”
- **현금으로 할게요** (*hyeongeumeuro halgeyo*) — “I’ll pay in cash.”
- **영수증 주세요** (*yeongsujeung juseyo*) — “The receipt, please.”
- **봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*) — “A bag, please.”

Before buying an item you may need to return, ask **교환 가능해요?** (*gyohwan ganeunghaeyo?*), “Is an exchange possible?” and **환불 가능해요?** (*hwanbul ganeunghaeyo?*), “Is a refund possible?” These questions do not promise that either is allowed. Keep the receipt and follow the store’s stated deadline and merchandise conditions.

You: **교환 가능해요?** — *gyohwan ganeunghaeyo?* — “Is an exchange possible?”  
Them: **영수증이 있어야 해요.** — *yeongsujeungi isseoya haeyo.* — “You need to have the receipt.”  
You: **네, 영수증 주세요.** — *ne, yeongsujeung juseyo.* — “Okay, the receipt, please.”

## A Seven-Step Clothing Store Script

1. Greet the staff with **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*), “Hello.”
2. Browse with **그냥 구경하고 있어요** (*geunyang gugyeonghago isseoyo*).
3. Ask for an option: **다른 사이즈 있어요?** (*dareun saijeu isseoyo?*).
4. Request a fitting: **입어 봐도 돼요?** (*ibeo bwado dwaeyo?*).
5. Check the fit: **잘 맞아요** (*jal majayo*), “It fits well.”
6. Confirm the purchase: **이걸로 주세요** (*igeollo juseyo*).
7. Ask for the receipt: **영수증 주세요** (*yeongsujeung juseyo*).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I say when I want to try on clothes in Korea?

Ask **이거 입어 봐도 돼요?** (*igeo ibeo bwado dwaeyo?*), “Can I try this on?” Wait for the staff to show you the fitting room or explain the store’s policy.

### How do I ask for a different size in Korean?

Say **다른 사이즈 있어요?** (*dareun saijeu isseoyo?*), “Do you have another size?” For a larger one, ask **더 큰 사이즈 있어요?** (*deo keun saijeu isseoyo?*).

### Should I ask for a discount in every Korean clothing store?

No. Treat posted prices as fixed unless a promotion is advertised or the seller says that negotiation is possible. Even stores inside traditional markets may use fixed prices.

### How do I say “I am just looking” in Korean?

Say **그냥 구경하고 있어요** (*geunyang gugyeonghago isseoyo*), “I’m just browsing.” It politely tells the staff that you do not need help yet.

### What if I do not understand the price?

Say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Please say that again,” or ask **적어 주세요** (*jeogeo juseyo*), “Please write it down.”

## Practice the Whole Shopping Sequence

Real shopping requires more than asking a price. Rehearse browsing, requesting a size, asking to try it on, describing the fit, and confirming the purchase as one short sequence.
