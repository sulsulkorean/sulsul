To meet new friends like in a K-drama, use phrases such as **반가워요** — *bangawoyo* — "Nice to meet you" and **잘 지냈어요?** — *jal jinaesseoyo?* — "How have you been?" These expressions help you start conversations naturally and are often used in K-dramas when characters meet someone new or reconnect with friends.

> - **반가워요** — *bangawoyo* — "Nice to meet you." Use it when meeting someone for the first time.
> - **잘 지냈어요?** — *jal jinaesseoyo?* — "How have you been?" Use it when catching up with someone.
> - **저는 [Your Name]이에요/예요** — *jeoneun [Your Name]ieyo/yeyo* — "I am [Your Name]." Use it to introduce yourself.
> - **어디서 오셨어요?** — *eodiseo osyeosseoyo?* — "Where are you from?" Use it to learn more about someone.
> - **연락처 교환할까요?** — *yeollakcheo gyohwanhalkkayo?* — "Shall we exchange contact details?" Use it to stay in touch.

## What do you actually say at a Korean cafe counter?

When meeting someone new at a cafe, you might start with a friendly greeting. 

**안녕하세요** — *annyeonghaseyo*
"Hello"
Literal: "Peace"
Use it when: Greeting someone politely in any setting.

After the greeting, you can introduce yourself.

**저는 [Your Name]이에요/예요** — *jeoneun [Your Name]ieyo/yeyo*
"I am [Your Name]"
Literal: "I am [Your Name]"
Use it when: Introducing yourself.

You: **안녕하세요** — *annyeonghaseyo* — "Hello"
Them: **안녕하세요** — *annyeonghaseyo* — "Hello"
You: **저는 [Your Name]이에요/예요** — *jeoneun [Your Name]ieyo/yeyo* — "I am [Your Name]"

## How to ask about someone's background?

To get to know someone better, you can ask about their background.

**어디서 오셨어요?** — *eodiseo osyeosseoyo?*
"Where are you from?"
Literal: "Where did you come from?"
Use it when: You want to learn about someone's origin.

You: **어디서 오셨어요?** — *eodiseo osyeosseoyo?* — "Where are you from?"
Them: **저는 서울에서 왔어요** — *jeoneun seouleseo wasseoyo* — "I'm from Seoul"
You: **서울은 정말 멋진 곳이에요** — *seoureun jeongmal meotjin gosieyo* — "Seoul is a really cool place"

## How to keep a conversation going?

To keep the conversation lively, ask about recent activities.

**요즘 어떻게 지내요?** — *yojeum eotteoke jinaeyo?*
"How have you been lately?"
Literal: "These days, how are you spending time?"
Use it when: Continuing a friendly conversation.

You: **요즘 어떻게 지내요?** — *yojeum eotteoke jinaeyo?* — "How have you been lately?"
Them: **잘 지내고 있어요** — *jal jinaego isseoyo* — "I've been doing well"
You: **좋네요! 저도 잘 지내고 있어요** — *jonneyo! jeodo jal jinaego isseoyo* — "That's good! I've been doing well too"

## How to ask about hobbies and interests?

Finding common interests can deepen a new friendship.

**취미가 뭐예요?** — *chwimiga mwoyeyo?*
"What are your hobbies?"
Literal: "Hobby what is?"
Use it when: You're interested in someone's hobbies.

You: **취미가 뭐예요?** — *chwimiga mwoyeyo?* — "What are your hobbies?"
Them: **저는 음악 듣기를 좋아해요** — *jeoneun eumak deudgireul joahaeyo* — "I like listening to music"
You: **저도 음악을 좋아해요!** — *jeodo eumageul joahaeyo!* — "I like music too!"

## How to exchange contact details?

If you want to stay in touch, propose exchanging contact information.

**연락처 교환할까요?** — *yeollakcheo gyohwanhalkkayo?*
"Shall we exchange contact details?"
Literal: "Contact exchange shall we?"
Use it when: You wish to continue the friendship.

You: **연락처 교환할까요?** — *yeollakcheo gyohwanhalkkayo?* — "Shall we exchange contact details?"
Them: **좋아요** — *joayo* — "Sounds good"
You: **여기 제 번호예요** — *yeogi je beonho-yeyo* — "Here's my number"

## How to say goodbye?

When parting ways, you can express your hope to meet again.

**다음에 또 봐요** — *da-eume tto bwayo*
"See you next time"
Literal: "Next time again see"
Use it when: Saying goodbye with the intention of meeting again.

You: **다음에 또 봐요** — *da-eume tto bwayo* — "See you next time"
Them: **네, 또 봐요** — *ne, tto bwayo* — "Yes, see you again"

## What usually goes wrong?

A common mistake is using overly formal language in casual settings. Instead of **안녕하십니까** — *annyeonghasimnikka*, stick with **안녕하세요** — *annyeonghaseyo* for a friendlier tone. 

Another mistake is forgetting to listen to the other person's response. Always be attentive to what they say to keep the conversation natural.

## How to compliment someone?

Compliments can make a positive impression and are often appreciated.

**멋져요!** — *meotjyeoyo!*
"You're awesome!"
Literal: "Awesome!"
Use it when: You want to compliment someone genuinely.

You: **멋져요!** — *meotjyeoyo!* — "You're awesome!"
Them: **고마워요** — *gomawoyo* — "Thank you"
You: **정말로요, 당신은 대단해요** — *jeongmallo-yo, dangsineun daedanhaeyo* — "Really, you're amazing"

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are some K-drama phrases for meeting new friends?

Some K-drama phrases for meeting new friends include '반가워요' for 'Nice to meet you' and '잘 지냈어요?' for 'How have you been?' These help you start conversations naturally.

### How do I say 'Nice to meet you' in Korean?

In Korean, 'Nice to meet you' is '반가워요'. Use it when meeting someone for the first time.

### What is a common question to ask a new friend in Korean?

A common question is '잘 지냈어요?' which means 'How have you been?' It shows interest in the person's well-being.

### How can I introduce myself in Korean?

To introduce yourself, say '저는 [Your Name]이에요/예요' which means 'I am [Your Name]'. Use '이에요' if your name ends in a consonant and '예요' if it ends in a vowel.
