The jimjilbang (*찜질방*, *jjimjilbang*) is Korea's all-in-one sauna, bath house, and rest space — open around the clock, cheap, and always full of locals. You pay at a front desk, receive a wristband and a uniform, then move freely between heated rooms, cold pools, and a food counter. The only thing standing between you and a perfect visit is knowing what to say at each of those stops.

> - Pay at the counter: **성인 한 명이요** (*seong-in han myeong-iyo*), "One adult, please."
> - Locate your locker: **사물함이 어디예요?** (*samuham-i eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the locker?"
> - Request a towel: **수건 주세요** (*sugeon juseyo*), "A towel, please."
> - Order the classic snack: **식혜 주세요** (*sikhye juseyo*), "Sikhye, please."

## What do you say when you first walk in?

The entry desk moves fast. Hand over your fee, say **성인 한 명이요** (*seong-in han myeong-iyo*), and you will receive a wristband with a number — that number links to every purchase you make inside. For two people, switch to **두 명이요** (*du myeong-iyo*), "Two people."

The staff may ask **남성이세요, 여성이세요?** (*namseong-iseyo, yeoseong-iseyo?*), "Male or female?" — the bathing areas are always gender-separated. Answer **여성이요** (*yeoseong-iyo*) or **남성이요** (*namseong-iyo*).

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| One adult entry | **성인 한 명이요** (*seong-in han myeong-iyo*) |
| Two people | **두 명이요** (*du myeong-iyo*) |
| Female section | **여성이요** (*yeoseong-iyo*) |
| Request a towel | **수건 주세요** (*sugeon juseyo*) |

## How do you find your way around inside?

Every jimjilbang has a heated room floor, a bathing area with pools, showers, and a lounge — sometimes on different floors. The phrase **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*) means "Where is it?" — drop the name of the place in front of it.

**탕 어디예요?** (*tang eodiyeyo?*) — "Where are the hot pools?"  
**샤워실 어디예요?** (*syawosil eodiyeyo?*) — "Where are the showers?"  
**찜질방 공간이 어디예요?** (*jjimjilbang gong-gan-i eodiyeyo?*) — "Where is the heated room area?"

If you are already changed and cannot find the rest area, any staff member in the gray uniform will point you in the right direction.

## How do you order food at the food counter?

The in-house food area is one of the best parts of a jimjilbang visit. **식혜** (*sikhye*) is a cold, sweet rice drink — order it by name. **구운 달걀** (*gu-un dal-gyal*) are eggs baked directly in the heated room, salty and oddly perfect.

Here is how the exchange usually goes:

You: **식혜 하나하고 구운 달걀 주세요.** — *sikhye hanahago gu-un dal-gyal juseyo.* — "Sikhye and a baked egg, please."  
Them: **손목 밴드로 계산할까요?** — *sonmok baendeu-ro gyesan-halkkayo?* — "Shall I charge it to your wristband?"  
You: **네, 그렇게 해 주세요.** — *ne, geureoke hae juseyo.* — "Yes, please."

When you leave, settle everything at the front desk by saying **계산해 주세요** (*gyesanhae juseyo*), "Please check me out."

## What phrases help if you stay overnight?

Many jimjilbang locations are open 24 hours and let guests sleep on heated floor mats in the rest area. If you want a blanket, ask **이불 빌려주실 수 있어요?** (*ibul billyeojusil su isseoyo?*), "Can I borrow a blanket?"

There are no wake-up calls — set your own alarm. When you leave in the morning, return the key at the front desk and say **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), "Thank you." The staff will confirm the bill and return the shoes you stored at entry.
