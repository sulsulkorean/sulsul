To see a doctor in Korea, walk in and say **아파서 왔어요** (*apaseo wasseoyo*), "I came because I'm in pain." The nurse or doctor then asks **어디가 아파요?** (*eodiga apayo?*), "Where does it hurt?" — answer with body part plus **아파요** (*apayo*), like **배가 아파요** (*baega apayo*), "My stomach hurts."

> - Open with **아파서 왔어요** (*apaseo wasseoyo*), "I came because I'm in pain," at the reception desk.
> - Answer **어디가 아파요?** (*eodiga apayo?*) with body part plus **아파요** (*apayo*), "It hurts."
> - Say **열이 나요** (*yeori nayo*), "I have a fever," if your temperature is high.
> - If the reply is too fast, say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), "Please say that again."

## How do you tell a doctor where it hurts?

Korean does not always need "my" before a body part. **머리가 아파요** (*meoriga apayo*) means "My head hurts" — the **가** (*ga*) marks the subject, so **나의** (*naui*, "my") is optional.

Point if you need to. **여기가 아파요** (*yeogiga apayo*), "It hurts here," works when the pain spot is hard to name.

| Symptom | Say this |
|---|---|
| Headache | 머리가 아파요 (*meoriga apayo*) |
| Stomachache | 배가 아파요 (*baega apayo*) |
| Sore throat | 목이 아파요 (*mogi apayo*) |
| Fever | 열이 나요 (*yeori nayo*) |
| Nausea | 속이 안 좋아요 (*sogi an joayo*) |

You do not need a long medical history at the front desk. One clear symptom line is enough for triage staff to send you to the right room.

## What happens once you sit down with the doctor?

Doctors in Korea often ask quick follow-up questions before prescribing anything. Listen for **언제부터 아팠어요?** (*eonjebuteo apasseoyo?*), "Since when has it hurt?"

You: **어제부터 배가 아파요.** — *eojebuteo baega apayo.* — "My stomach has hurt since yesterday."  
Them: **열은 있어요?** — *yeoreun isseoyo?* — "Do you have a fever?"  
You: **조금 있어요.** — *jogeum isseoyo.* — "A little."

**조금** (*jogeum*) means "a little" — use it for mild fever or pain instead of guessing numbers in Celsius.

## How do you ask about medicine and follow-up?

After the exam, the doctor explains what to take and whether you need a return visit. Ask **약은 어떻게 먹어요?** (*yageun eotteoke meogeoyo?*), "How do I take the medicine?" if the instructions come too fast.

**며칠 동안 먹어요?** (*myeochil dongan meogeoyo?*) means "How many days do I take it?" — useful when you are packing for a short trip and cannot come back easily.

If symptoms feel serious — trouble breathing, chest pain, or a high fever that will not drop — say **응급실에 가야 해요?** (*eungguksire gaya haeyo?*), "Do I need to go to the emergency room?" Staff will point you to the right wing.

## Can you prepare these lines before you travel?

Most travelers rehearse **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*) and freeze the moment a nurse asks **어디가 아파요?** That reply is the half worth practicing, not the greeting.
