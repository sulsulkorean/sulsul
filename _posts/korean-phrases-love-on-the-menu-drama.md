**Love on the Menu** is riding one of the strongest K-drama ratings runs this summer — and the Korean around the table is plain praise, not chef jargon. Tell your host **정말 맛있어요** (*jeongmal masisseoyo*), "It is really delicious," or ask for another round with **한 번 더 주세요** (*han beon deo juseyo*), "One more, please," when a side dish disappears before the main course arrives.

> - Praise the food: **정말 맛있어요** (*jeongmal masisseoyo*), "It is really delicious."
> - Order seconds: **한 번 더 주세요** (*han beon deo juseyo*), "One more, please."
> - Ask what is good: **뭐가 제일 맛있어요?** (*mwoga jeil masisseoyo?*), "What is the tastiest?"
> - Offer to treat: **제가 살게요** (*jega salgeyo*), "I will pay."

## How do you talk about food the way drama characters do?

You do not need kitchen vocabulary. Viewers repeat short lines about taste and sharing.

1. When a dish lands, say **정말 맛있어요** before you reach for your phone.
2. Ask **뭐가 제일 맛있어요?** if the menu is all in Korean.
3. When everyone agrees, add **저도요** (*jeodoyo*), "Me too."
4. If a banchan is empty, try **한 번 더 주세요** with a smile.
5. Close the meal with **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeotseumnida*), "I ate well."

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| First bite praise | 정말 맛있어요 (*jeongmal masisseoyo*) |
| Pick a dish | 뭐가 제일 맛있어요? (*mwoga jeil masisseoyo?*) |
| Agree with a friend | 저도요 (*jeodoyo*) |
| Refill a side | 한 번 더 주세요 (*han beon deo juseyo*) |
| Polite goodbye | 잘 먹었습니다 (*jal meogeotseumnida*) |

## What does a Korean friend say when you praise the meal?

Replies stay warm and short.

You: **오늘 음식 정말 맛있어요.** — *oneul eumsik jeongmal masisseoyo.* — "Today's food is really delicious."  
Them: **다행이에요, 더 드세요.** — *dahaengieyo, deo deuseyo.* — "Good, please eat more."  
You: **한 번 더 주세요.** — *han beon deo juseyo.* — "One more, please."

If someone offers to pay, **괜찮아요** (*gwaenchanayo*), "It is okay," lets you decline politely before **제가 살게요** wins the moment.

## What usually goes wrong when drama dinner lines meet real restaurants?

Learners memorize dramatic confessions but never practice **정말 맛있어요**, which is what servers and friends actually hear at a table.

A second mistake is shouting English-only reactions in a room where everyone else switches to Korean for one sentence — you do not need fluency, just one clear praise line. For more ordering help, see [Korean phrases for ordering at a restaurant](/korean-phrases-ordering-restaurant-korea).
