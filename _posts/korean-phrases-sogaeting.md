Soompi's drama coverage has tracked 소개팅 (*sogaeting*) — the Korean blind date set up by a mutual friend — as one of the most recurring meet-cute situations in Korean romance series, from tvN to JTBC. The reason writers keep using it is simple: the first meeting has a clear structure, and that structure produces good phrases. The same phrases that open a 소개팅 scene in a drama are the ones you need when you actually meet someone new in Korea.

> - Greet them: **만나서 반가워요.** (*mannaseo ban-gawoyo.*), "Nice to meet you."
> - Introduce yourself: **저는 [이름]이에요.** (*jeoneun [ireum]ieyo.*), "I'm [name]."
> - Suggest a drink: **뭐 드실래요?** (*mwo deusillaeyo?*), "What would you like to have?"
> - Wrap up well: **오늘 정말 즐거웠어요.** (*oneul jeongmal jeulgeowosseoyo.*), "Today was really fun."

## How does the opening exchange usually go?

A 소개팅 starts with the greeting, not a question. Lead with **만나서 반가워요** and your name — the other person mirrors it. Both sides know the format, so there is no awkwardness about who speaks first.

You: **만나서 반가워요. 저는 민준이에요.** — *mannaseo ban-gawoyo. jeoneun minjunieyeo.* — "Nice to meet you. I'm Minjun."  
Them: **저도요. 저는 지은이에요.** — *jeodoyo. jeoneun jieunieyeo.* — "Same here. I'm Jieun."  
You: **근처에 좋은 카페 알아요?** — *geuncheoe joheun kape arayo?* — "Do you know a good café nearby?"

**저도요** (*jeodoyo*) means "me too" or "same here" — it is the most natural way to echo what the other person said. Using it on a first meeting signals ease, not laziness.

## What do you say when you want to know more about them?

The early conversation is about finding overlap — food, music, the neighbourhood.

| Topic | Ask this |
|---|---|
| Food | 무슨 음식 좋아해요? (*museun eumsik joahaeyo?*) — "What food do you like?" |
| The area | 이 동네 자주 와요? (*i dongne jaju wayo?*) — "Do you come here often?" |
| A shared interest | 뭐 좋아해요? (*mwo joahaeyo?*) — "What do you like?" |
| Weekend plans | 주말에 뭐 해요? (*jumare mwo haeyo?*) — "What do you do on weekends?" |

Keep the first few questions open. A question that needs only a yes or no answer does not move the conversation forward. **뭐 좋아해요?** (*mwo joahaeyo?*) opens a door; **좋아해요?** (*joahaeyo?*) closes it.

## How do you suggest a second meeting without it being awkward?

The natural close is to name what you enjoyed and suggest it again.

**오늘 정말 즐거웠어요.** — *oneul jeongmal jeulgeowosseoyo.* — "Today was really fun."

Then, if you want to meet again: **또 봐요** (*tto bwayo*), "Let's see each other again." That phrasing is light and genuine — it leaves space for the other person to say yes or suggest a time. If they also had a good time, they will reply with **좋아요** (*joayo*), "Sounds good," or name a day.

If the date did not go as well, a polite exit is simply **조심히 들어가세요** (*josimhi deureogaseyo*), "Get home safely." It is warm without being misleading.

## What register should you use throughout?

Use polite **-요** speech for the entire first meeting. Dropping to casual speech (**반말**, *banmal*) on a first date signals too much familiarity too fast, even if the drama scene you watched did it. Wait until the other person drops **-요** first — or until you are both clearly comfortable enough to suggest it together.

**만나서 반가워요** is four syllables. If you have to think about it while the other person is standing in front of you, the pause is longer than you expect. Practice the opening line, the food question, and the close — those three cover ninety percent of the first meeting.
