Korean phrases for ordering at a restaurant cover three beats: getting seated, naming what you want, and asking for something you need mid-meal. Start with **두 명이에요** (*du myeongieyo*), "We are two people," at the door, then **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), "This one, please," when you point at the menu.

> - Tell the host your party size: **두 명이에요** (*du myeongieyo*), "We are two people."
> - Order from the menu: **이거 주세요** (*igeo juseyo*), "This one, please."
> - Ask for water: **물 좀 주세요** (*mul jom juseyo*), "Some water, please."
> - Request the bill: **계산서 주세요** (*gyesanseo juseyo*), "The bill, please."

## How do you get seated without guessing?

Most Korean restaurants greet you at the entrance before you walk to a table. The host needs a number, not a long story.

1. Say how many people: **세 명이에요** (*se myeongieyo*) for three, **혼자예요** (*honjayeoyo*) if you are alone.
2. Wait to be led to a table or follow the gesture toward an open seat.
3. If the menu is on the wall, read it while standing — many places expect you to order at the counter first.
4. Sit down and say **물 좀 주세요** if no water arrives within a minute.
5. When you are ready to pay, catch the staff eye and say **계산서 주세요**.

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Two people | 두 명이에요 (*du myeongieyo*) |
| Point and order | 이거 주세요 (*igeo juseyo*) |
| Not spicy | 안 매운 걸로 주세요 (*an maeun geollo juseyo*) |
| Extra side dish | 이거 더 주세요 (*igeo deo juseyo*) |
| Pay by card | 카드로 할게요 (*kadeuro halgeyo*) |

## What does the staff say back?

Replies stay short. Listen for **네** (*ne*), "yes," or a follow-up about spice level.

You: **두 명이에요.** — *du myeongieyo.* — "We are two people."  
Them: **이쪽으로 앉으세요.** — *ijjogeuro anjeuseyo.* — "Please sit this way."  
You: **감사합니다.** — *gamsahamnida.* — "Thank you."

If they ask **매운 거 괜찮으세요?** (*maeun geo gwaenchanseuseyo?*), "Is spicy food okay?" answer **네, 괜찮아요** (*ne, gwaenchanayo*) or **안 매운 걸로 주세요** (*an maeun geollo juseyo*). One clear answer beats a long explanation.

## What usually goes wrong at the table?

Learners often wait silently for someone to take an order when the staff expects you to call **여기요** (*yeogiyo*), a polite "excuse me" that gets attention without shouting. Practice it once before you need it.

A second mistake is mixing up **주세요** (*juseyo*), "please give me," with **할게요** (*halgeyo*), "I will do it" — **계산할게요** (*gyesanhalgeyo*) means "I will pay," while **계산서 주세요** asks for the bill.
