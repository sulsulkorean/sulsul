Chi-maek — **치맥** (*chimaek*), chicken plus beer — is how Korean friends unwind after work or before a drama binge, and the table moves faster when you can say **치킨 시켜요** (*chikin sikyeoyo*), "Order chicken, please," instead of silently pointing at the menu photo. If the first round disappears quickly, **맥주 한 잔 더 주세요** (*maekju han jan deo juseyo*), "One more beer, please," keeps the night rolling.

> - Call for chicken: **치킨 시켜요** (*chikin sikyeoyo*), "Order chicken, please."
> - Ask for another beer: **맥주 한 잔 더 주세요** (*maekju han jan deo juseyo*), "One more beer, please."
> - Pick a flavor: **양념치킨이요** (*yangnyeom chikinyo*), "Seasoned chicken, please."
> - Share the hype: **진짜 맛있어요** (*jinjja masisseoyo*), "It is really tasty."

## How do you order at a chi-maek table?

Korean chicken places expect short, direct lines — not full English sentences translated word for word.

1. When everyone sits down, say **치킨 시켜요** and point at the flavor board if needed.
2. For saucy wings, add **양념치킨이요**; for plain crunch, **후라이드요** (*peuraideuyo*), "Fried, please."
3. When glasses empty, raise a hand and say **맥주 한 잔 더 주세요**.
4. After the first bite, tell the group **진짜 맛있어요**.
5. Before the bill, ask **나눠서 낼까요?** (*nanwoseo nalkkayo?*), "Shall we split the bill?"

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Start the order | 치킨 시켜요 (*chikin sikyeoyo*) |
| Pick seasoned wings | 양념치킨이요 (*yangnyeom chikinyo*) |
| Refill drinks | 맥주 한 잔 더 주세요 (*maekju han jan deo juseyo*) |
| React to taste | 진짜 맛있어요 (*jinjja masisseoyo*) |
| Split the check | 나눠서 낼까요? (*nanwoseo nalkkayo?*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Staff and friends keep replies short — perfect for beginners.

You: **양념치킨이요.** — *yangnyeom chikinyo.* — "Seasoned chicken, please."  
Them: **네, 잠시만요!** — *ne, jamsimanyo!* — "Yes, one moment!"  
You: **맥주 한 잔 더 주세요.** — *maekju han jan deo juseyo.*

If a friend asks whether you want extra sauce, **네, 주세요** (*ne, juseyo*), "Yes, please," is enough. Match the energy, not the grammar chart.

## What usually goes wrong on chi-maek night?

Learners memorize **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*) but never practice **치킨 시켜요**, the line every chicken shop hears fifty times an hour.

A second mistake is staying quiet while friends switch to Korean for two seconds to order — one clear phrase keeps you in the room. For snack runs before the main tray arrives, see [Korean phrases at a convenience store](/korean-phrases-convenience-store).
