To greet someone in Korean, match the level to the relationship. Use **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*) with most adults you don't know well, casual **안녕** (*annyeong*) only with close friends, and formal **안녕하십니까** (*annyeonghasimnikka*) for ceremonies or broadcasts. **해요체** (*haeyoche*) is the safe default when you're unsure which one fits.

> - **안녕하세요.** (*annyeonghaseyo.*) is everyday polite speech, safe with unfamiliar adults.
> - **안녕.** (*annyeong.*) is casual — use it only once a friendship already allows casual speech.
> - **안녕하십니까.** (*annyeonghasimnikka.*), formal **하십시오체** (*hasipsioche*), also called **합쇼체** (*hapsyoche*), fits ceremonies and speeches.
> - Being the same age as someone does not automatically make casual speech appropriate.

## The three levels a beginner actually needs

Korean has more speech styles than this, but these three cover almost every beginner greeting.

| Speech style | Greeting |
|---|---|
| Casual (해체) | 안녕 *(annyeong)* |
| Everyday polite (해요체) | 안녕하세요 *(annyeonghaseyo)* |
| Formal polite (합쇼체) | 안녕하십니까 *(annyeonghasimnikka)* |
| First meeting (해요체) | 반가워요 *(bangawoyo)* |
| Parting (해요체) | 안녕히 가세요 *(annyeonghi gaseyo)* |

**해요체** is not a weaker version of **합쇼체** — they're two different registers for different moments, not a ladder based only on age or job title.

## Greet someone politely for the first time

For a classmate you just met, a neighbor, or a new coworker, **해요체** is almost always the right call.

You: **안녕하세요. 저는 마야예요.** — *annyeonghaseyo. jeoneun mayayeyo.* — "Hello. I'm Maya."  
Them: **반가워요. 저는 지훈이에요.** — *bangawoyo. jeoneun jihunieyo.* — "Nice to meet you. I'm Jihun."  
You: **저도 반가워요.** — *jeodo bangawoyo.* — "Nice to meet you too."

For a more formal first meeting, **처음 뵙겠습니다** (*cheoeum boepgetseumnida*), "It's a pleasure to meet you," lowers the speaker relative to the person being met.

## Open a formal event with 하십시오체

**합쇼체**, more precisely called **하십시오체**, has a crisp, public-facing tone. It fits an audience or a representative role, not an ordinary hallway greeting.

You: **안녕하십니까. 오늘 발표를 맡은 리나입니다.** — *annyeonghasimnikka. oneul balpyoreul mateun rinaimnida.* — "Good day. I'm Rina, today's presenter."  
Them: **네, 반갑습니다.** — *ne, bangapseumnida.* — "Yes, it's nice to meet you."

Repeating **하십시오체** in a casual office conversation can sound like an announcement. Save it for the moment that's actually formal, then drop back to **해요체** once the event ends.

## What usually goes wrong when choosing a level

The most common mistake is switching to **안녕** with someone your own age just because you're close in age — familiarity, not age, grants that permission. The opposite mistake is staying in **하십시오체** all day with a manager who actually speaks **해요체** in ordinary conversation, which can create unnecessary distance.

Watch for **오빠**, **언니**, **형**, and **누나** too: they depend on gender, relative age, and an accepted relationship, so a name or title like **선생님** (*seonsaengnim*) is safer at first.
