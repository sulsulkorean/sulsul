Korean phrases for KakaoTalk texting are shorter and faster than spoken Korean. Send **지금 가는 길이에요** (*jigeum ganeun girieyo*), "I am on my way," when a friend asks where you are. Reply **잠시만요** (*jamsimanyo*), "One moment," when you need thirty seconds before answering. Those two lines cover half the chats you will have in Korea.

> - Say you are coming: **지금 가는 길이에요** (*jigeum ganeun girieyo*), "I am on my way."
> - Ask them to wait: **잠시만요** (*jamsimanyo*), "One moment."
> - Confirm you arrived: **도착했어요** (*dochakhaesseoyo*), "I have arrived."
> - Say you are running late: **조금 늦을 것 같아요** (*jogeum neujeul geot gatayo*), "I think I will be a little late."

## How do you tell someone you are on your way?

KakaoTalk runs on quick status updates. **지금** (*jigeum*, now) + **가는 길이에요** (*ganeun girieyo*, on the way there) is the standard "OMW" message. If you are heading toward the person, use **오는 길이에요** (*oneun girieyo*), "I am on my way to you."

You: **벌써 출발했어요?** — *beolsseo chulbalhaesseoyo?* — "Did you already leave?"  
Them: **응, 지금 가는 길이에요.** — *eung, jigeum ganeun girieyo.* — "Yeah, I am on my way."  
You: **알겠어요. 천천히 오세요.** — *algessoyo. cheoncheonhi oseyo.* — "Got it. Take your time."

When you arrive, send **도착했어요** (*dochakhaesseoyo*) so they stop wondering. One word change, zero awkward "Are you here yet?" loops.

## What do you text when you are late or confused?

Running late is normal; saying nothing is what frustrates people. **조금 늦을 것 같아요** (*jogeum neujeul geot gatayo*) sets expectations without a long apology.

| Situation | Text this |
|---|---|
| Running late | 조금 늦을 것 같아요 (*jogeum neujeul geot gatayo*) |
| Need a minute | 잠시만요 (*jamsimanyo*) |
| Cannot make it | 오늘 못 가요 (*oneul mot gayo*) |
| Ask to reschedule | 다른 시간 괜찮아요? (*dareun sigan gwaenchanayo?*) |
| Say thanks after meeting | 오늘 재밌었어요 (*oneul jaebisseoyo*) |

If someone sends a voice note you cannot follow, **천천히 말해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo*), "Please say it slowly," works even in text.

## How do you react and keep the chat warm?

Short reactions carry a lot of weight. **그래요?** (*geuraeyo?*, "Really?") shows you read the message. **다행이에요** (*dahaengieyo*), "That is a relief," fits when someone says they found their wallet or finished a test.

You: **시험 끝났어요!** — *siheom kkeunnasseoyo!* — "The exam is over!"  
Them: **와, 다행이에요! 수고하셨어요.** — *wa, dahaengieyo! sugohasyeosseoyo.* — "Wow, that is a relief! Good job."  
You: **감사합니다. 저녁에 시간 있어요?** — *gamsahamnida. jeonyeoge sigan isseoyo?* — "Thank you. Do you have time this evening?"
