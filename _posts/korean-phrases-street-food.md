Eater's Seoul city guides have put 포장마차 (*pojangmacha*) street stalls — the folding tents serving 떡볶이 (*tteokbokki*), 순대 (*sundae*), and 오뎅 (*odeng*) — on first-day itineraries for years. The food is fast, the portions are shareable, and the ordering takes two sentences if you know them. The phrases below work at any stall, whether the cook stands under a roadside tent near the Han River or runs a counter inside a traditional market hall.

> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"
> - Order one portion: **하나 주세요.** (*hana juseyo.*), "One, please."
> - Ask for less spice: **덜 맵게 해주세요.** (*deol maepge haejuseyo.*), "Less spicy, please."
> - Ask to eat there: **여기서 먹어도 돼요?** (*yeogiseo meogeoyo?*), "May I eat here?"

## How do you order tteokbokki without pointing?

Most stalls display the food in front of you, so you can point — but one spoken sentence gets faster service and lets you ask for a spice adjustment.

You: **떡볶이 하나 주세요.** — *tteokbokki hana juseyo.* — "One tteokbokki, please."  
Them: **맵게 드릴까요?** — *maepge deurilkkayo?* — "Shall I make it spicy?"  
You: **조금만 맵게요.** — *jogeumman maepgeyo.* — "Just a little spicy."

**드릴까요?** (*deurilkkayo?*) is the polite offer "shall I give you?" — you will hear it at almost every street stall. Answer with the option plus **요**: **맵게요** (*maepgeyo*, spicy), **안 맵게요** (*an maepgeyo*, not spicy), or **조금만 맵게요** (*jogeumman maepgeyo*, just a little spicy).

## What are the most useful ordering phrases at a street stall?

Street food ordering follows the same pattern every time: name the item, say a number, add **주세요**. What changes is the customization.

| What you want | Say this |
|---|---|
| Less spicy | 덜 맵게 해주세요. (*deol maepge haejuseyo.*) |
| Extra sauce | 소스 더 주세요. (*soseu deo juseyo.*) |
| A bag to carry it | 봉투 주세요. (*bongtu juseyo.*) |
| Eat at the counter | 여기서 먹어도 돼요? (*yeogiseo meogeoyo?*) |

**봉투 주세요** (*bongtu juseyo*) asks for a carrier bag when you want to walk and eat. Most stalls assume you will stand at the counter unless you ask otherwise.

## What do you say after the first bite?

Korean street food vendors care about their sauce and broth. A single honest compliment lands well.

**맛있어요!** — *masisseoyo!* — "It's delicious!"

Follow it with **뭐예요?** (*mwoyeyo?*), "What is this?" if something in the bowl looks unfamiliar. Vendors enjoy explaining their food, and you will often get the name of an ingredient plus a small sample of something else on the counter.

## How do you pay and ask for the bill?

Most street stalls accept card alongside cash. When you finish, say **계산해 주세요** (*gyesanhae juseyo*), "The bill, please." The vendor will say the total aloud. If you missed the number, ask **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*) again — it works for the total just as well as for the initial price check.

## Say the order out loud before the queue moves

Standing at a busy tteokbokki stall with a line behind you is not the moment to rehearse. Practice **하나 주세요** and **덜 맵게 해주세요** before you arrive — they are short enough to say without thinking once you have spoken them a few times.
