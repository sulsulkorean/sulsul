The safest, most natural compliment for a Korean friend’s cooking is **정말 맛있어요** (*jeongmal masisseoyo*), “It’s really delicious.” To make it sound personal rather than memorized, add one detail you honestly noticed: the seasoning, texture, aroma, or a particular dish.

You can use the polite **-요** style with a friend, a friend’s family, or someone you have just met. If you and your friend normally speak casually, **맛있어** (*masisseo*) is the casual equivalent. When in doubt, **맛있어요** is warm without being too familiar.

> **Quick answer:** Say **맛이 훌륭해요** (*masi hullyunghaeyo*), “It tastes excellent,” then add a specific line such as **간이 딱 좋아요** (*gani ttak joayo*), “The seasoning is just right.” After eating, say **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*) to thank the person for the meal.

## Start with a genuine first reaction

**맛있어요** literally describes something as delicious. Korean often leaves out “this” or “it” when the subject is obvious, so the two-word English sentence “It’s delicious” becomes one Korean word.

- **맛있어요.** — *masisseoyo.* — “It’s delicious.”
- **정말 맛있어요.** — *jeongmal masisseoyo.* — “It’s really delicious.”
- **진짜 맛있어요.** — *jinjja masisseoyo.* — “It’s seriously/really delicious.”
- **향이 정말 좋아요.** — *hyangi jeongmal joayo.* — “The aroma is really nice.”
- **보기에도 맛있어 보여요.** — *bogiedo masisseo boyeoyo.* — “It looks delicious too.”

**정말** works in almost any polite setting. **진짜** is conversational and enthusiastic, so it fits relaxed meals with friends. Neither word can make an insincere compliment convincing; your expression and the detail that follows matter more.

At the first bite, a simple exchange may be:

You: **와, 정말 맛있어요!** — *wa, jeongmal masisseoyo!* — “Wow, it’s really delicious!”  
Them: **다행이네요. 입에 맞아요?** — *dahaengineyo. ibe majayo?* — “I’m glad. Is it to your taste?”  
You: **네, 간이 딱 좋아요.** — *ne, gani ttak joayo.* — “Yes, the seasoning is just right.”

**입에 맞아요?** literally asks whether the food “fits your mouth,” and naturally means “Is it to your taste?” You can answer **네, 입에 잘 맞아요** (*ne, ibe jal majayo*), “Yes, it suits my taste.”

## Make the compliment specific

Instead of repeatedly saying **맛있어요**, choose one feature. This makes the conversation more useful for a beginner and gives the cook a natural opening to respond.

| What you noticed | Korean | Revised Romanization | Natural meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seasoning | **간이 딱 좋아요** | *gani ttak joayo* | The seasoning is just right |
| Texture | **식감이 좋아요** | *sikgami joayo* | The texture is good |
| Savory, nutty flavor | **고소해요** | *gosohaeyo* | It’s savory and nutty |
| Clean, mild flavor | **담백해요** | *dambaekhaeyo* | It has a clean, mild taste |
| Pleasant spice | **매콤해서 좋아요** | *maekomhaeseo joayo* | I like that it’s pleasantly spicy |
| Tender texture | **고기가 정말 부드러워요** | *gogiga jeongmal budeureowoyo* | The meat is really tender |
| A favorite | **이게 제일 맛있어요** | *ige jeil masisseoyo* | I like this one best |

Flavor words do not map perfectly between languages. **고소하다** can describe the appealing roasted or nutty quality of sesame, nuts, toasted grains, or some rich foods. **담백하다** often praises food that tastes clean and not overly heavy. Use these words only when they match what you are eating.

## Compliment a dish and ask about it

Naming the dish is more natural than saying **이 요리가** (“this dish”) again and again. If you know the name, say **잡채가 특히 맛있어요** (*japchaega teukhi masisseoyo*), “The japchae is especially delicious.” If you do not know it, point and ask.

You: **이게 뭐예요? 진짜 맛있어요.** — *ige mwoyeyo? jinjja masisseoyo.* — “What is this? It’s seriously delicious.”  
Them: **감자전이에요. 제가 만들었어요.** — *gamjajeonieyo. jega mandeureosseoyo.* — “It’s a potato pancake. I made it.”  
You: **직접 만들었어요? 식감이 정말 좋아요.** — *jikjeop mandeureosseoyo? sikgami jeongmal joayo.* — “You made it yourself? The texture is really good.”

Useful follow-up questions include:

- **이거 직접 만들었어요?** — *igeo jikjeop mandeureosseoyo?* — “Did you make this yourself?”
- **어떻게 만들었어요?** — *eotteoke mandeureosseoyo?* — “How did you make it?”
- **뭐가 들어갔어요?** — *mwoga deureogasseoyo?* — “What went into it?”
- **레시피를 알려 줄 수 있어요?** — *resipireul allyeo jul su isseoyo?* — “Could you share the recipe?”

Ask about ingredients when you are curious, but state allergies or dietary needs directly rather than relying on a casual recipe conversation. A compliment is not a substitute for checking what you can safely eat.

## Praise effort without making assumptions

Some compliments in the rejected draft claimed that ingredients were fresh or that a meal was “full of sincerity.” Those can sound unnatural if you cannot actually know the ingredients or the cook’s process. It is better to praise something observable or thank the person for the time they spent.

Try:

- **많이 준비했네요.** — *mani junbihaenneyo.* — “You prepared so much.”
- **정말 정성스럽게 만들었네요.** — *jeongmal jeongseureopge mandeureonneyo.* — “You made it with such care.”
- **초대해 줘서 고마워요.** — *chodaehae jwoseo gomawoyo.* — “Thank you for inviting me.”
- **요리하느라 고생했어요.** — *yorihaneura gosaenghaesseoyo.* — “You worked hard cooking.”

**-네요** adds the feeling of noticing or realizing something: **많이 준비했네요** conveys “I see you prepared a lot.” It is well suited to a sincere observation at the table.

You: **많이 준비했네요. 요리하느라 고생했어요.** — *mani junbihaenneyo. yorihaneura gosaenghaesseoyo.* — “You prepared so much. You worked hard cooking.”  
Them: **괜찮아요. 같이 먹고 싶었어요.** — *gwaenchanayo. gachi meokgo sipeosseoyo.* — “It’s okay. I wanted to eat together.”  
You: **초대해 줘서 정말 고마워요.** — *chodaehae jwoseo jeongmal gomawoyo.* — “Thank you so much for inviting me.”

This exchange responds to what the friend actually says; it does not assume that every cook will reject a compliment or answer modestly.

## Use before-meal and after-meal thanks correctly

Before eating, **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgetseumnida*) literally has the sense of “I will eat well.” In practice, it acknowledges the meal you are about to receive. After eating, **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*) means “I ate well” and functions as thanks for the meal.

These expressions are not direct translations of “bon appétit.” The person receiving the food says them. With a close friend, the softer polite forms **잘 먹을게요** (*jal meogeulgeyo*), “I’ll enjoy it,” and **잘 먹었어요** (*jal meogeosseoyo*), “I enjoyed the meal,” also sound natural.

Use this simple sequence:

1. When the food is served, say **잘 먹겠습니다**.
2. Taste before giving a detailed opinion.
3. React with **맛있네요** (*masinneyo*), “This is delicious,” if you mean it.
4. Mention one specific feature, such as **간이 딱 좋아요**.
5. Ask one natural follow-up if you are curious.
6. At the end, say **잘 먹었습니다** and thank the host.

At the end of dinner:

You: **잘 먹었습니다. 특히 잡채가 정말 맛있었어요.** — *jal meogeosseumnida. teukhi japchaega jeongmal masisseosseoyo.* — “Thank you for the meal. The japchae was especially delicious.”  
Them: **맛있게 먹어 줘서 고마워요.** — *masitge meogeo jwoseo gomawoyo.* — “Thank you for enjoying it.”  
You: **다음에 만드는 법도 알려 주세요.** — *daeume mandeuneun beopdo allyeo juseyo.* — “Next time, please show me how to make it too.”

The past form **맛있었어요** (*masisseosseoyo*) fits naturally once the meal is over.

## Avoid compliments that land the wrong way

Adding a negative comparison can weaken praise. **생각보다 맛있어요** (*saenggakboda masisseoyo*) means “It’s tastier than I expected,” which may imply low expectations. **한국 사람처럼 잘 만들었어요** (“You made it well like a Korean person”) makes an unnecessary identity comparison. Say what tastes good instead.

Be careful with **안 매워서 좋아요** (*an maewoseo joayo*), “I like it because it isn’t spicy.” It is fine when the cook knows that you avoid spicy food, but it is not automatically praise for every dish. **매콤해서 좋아요**, “I like the pleasant spice,” is equally specific in the opposite direction.

Also avoid declaring **최고예요** (*choegoyeyo*), “It’s the best,” after every bite. It is grammatically correct, but detail sounds more believable. A useful beginner formula is:

**[Dish] + 특히 맛있어요. [Feature] + 좋아요.**

For example: **불고기 맛이 훌륭해요. 고기가 부드러워요.** (*bulgogi masi hullyunghaeyo. gogiga budeureowoyo.*), “The bulgogi tastes excellent. The meat is tender.”

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you say "It's delicious" in Korean?

Say **맛있어요** (*masisseoyo*). Add **정말** for “really”: **정말 맛있어요**.

### What is a polite way to compliment a Korean friend's cooking?

Say **정말 맛있어요**, then mention something you genuinely liked: **간이 딱 좋아요** (“The seasoning is just right”) or **식감이 좋아요** (“The texture is good”).

### How can I express gratitude for a meal in Korean?

Before eating, say **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgetseumnida*). After the meal, say **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*) to thank the person who provided it.

### What phrase can I use to compliment a specific dish?

Name the food: **잡채가 특히 맛있어요** means “The japchae is especially delicious.” If you do not know its name, point and say **이게 제일 맛있어요**, “I like this one best.”
