To compliment a Korean friend's cooking, say **정말 맛있어요** (*jeongmal masisseoyo*), "It's really delicious." Add one detail you actually noticed, like **간이 딱 좋아요** (*gani ttak joayo*), "The seasoning is just right." The polite **-요** ending works whether this is a new friend or someone you know well.

> - **정말 맛있어요.** (*jeongmal masisseoyo.*) is the safest opener: "It's really delicious."
> - **간이 딱 좋아요.** (*gani ttak joayo.*) praises the seasoning specifically.
> - Before eating, say **잘 먹겠습니다.** (*jal meokgetseumnida.*); after, say **잘 먹었습니다.** (*jal meogeosseumnida.*) to thank the cook.
> - Skip **최고예요** (*choegoyeyo*) after every bite — one specific compliment beats five repeated ones.

## Start with a genuine first reaction

**맛있어요** (*masisseoyo*) simply means "delicious." Korean often drops "this" or "it" when the subject is obvious, so the two-word English sentence becomes one Korean word. **정말** fits almost any polite setting, while **진짜** feels more conversational and enthusiastic for a relaxed meal with friends.

Neither word rescues an insincere compliment on its own — your tone and the detail that follows matter more than the intensifier you pick.

## Name the specific thing you noticed

Repeating **맛있어요** on its own gets thin fast. Naming one feature gives the cook something real to respond to, and it makes you sound like you actually tasted the food.

| What you noticed | Say this |
|---|---|
| First bite | 정말 맛있어요. *(jeongmal masisseoyo.)* |
| Seasoning | 간이 딱 좋아요. *(gani ttak joayo.)* |
| Texture | 식감이 좋아요. *(sikgami joayo.)* |
| Before the meal | 잘 먹겠습니다. *(jal meokgetseumnida.)* |
| After the meal | 잘 먹었습니다. *(jal meogeosseumnida.)* |

## Handle the reply, not just the compliment

The part that actually stalls learners is the question that comes back, not the opening compliment.

You: **와, 정말 맛있어요!** — *wa, jeongmal masisseoyo!* — "Wow, it's really delicious!"  
Them: **다행이에요. 입에 맞아요?** — *dahaengieyo. ibe majayo?* — "I'm glad. Is it to your taste?"  
You: **네, 간이 딱 좋아요.** — *ne, gani ttak joayo.* — "Yes, the seasoning is just right."

**입에 맞아요?** (*ibe majayo?*) literally asks whether the food "fits your mouth," and naturally means "Is it to your taste?" A simple **네** plus one specific detail answers it fully.

## Thank the cook before and after the meal

Before eating, **잘 먹겠습니다** (*jal meokgetseumnida*) carries the sense of "I will eat well," acknowledging the meal you're about to receive. After eating, **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeosseumnida*) means "I ate well" and works as thanks for the whole meal, not just the last bite.

What usually goes wrong: learners say **최고예요** ("it's the best") after every single bite until it stops meaning anything. A cook notices one honest, specific line — the seasoning, the texture, a dish you'd ask about again — far more than five repeated superlatives.
