Korean summer street food stalls stay open late — and the lines move faster when you can say **이거 하나 주세요** (*igeo hana juseyo*), "One of this, please," instead of only pointing. If the tteokbokki hits harder than expected, **너무 매워요** (*neomu maewoyo*), "It is too spicy," tells the vendor exactly what you need.

> - Point and order: **이거 하나 주세요** (*igeo hana juseyo*), "One of this, please."
> - Say it is spicy: **너무 매워요** (*neomu maewoyo*), "It is too spicy."
> - Ask the price: **얼마예요?** (*eolmayeyo?*), "How much is it?"
> - Say it is good: **진짜 맛있어요** (*jinjja masisseoyo*), "It is really tasty."

## How do you order at a crowded summer stall?

Stalls reward short, clear Korean — not full sentences.

1. Point at the tray and say **이거 하나 주세요**.
2. If the sign has no English, ask **얼마예요?**
3. When the heat builds, say **너무 매워요** and ask for water: **물 좀 주세요** (*mul jom juseyo*), "Water, please."
4. After the first bite, tell a friend **진짜 맛있어요**.
5. Before you leave, say **잘 먹었습니다** (*jal meogeotseumnida*), "I ate well."

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| Order one item | 이거 하나 주세요 (*igeo hana juseyo*) |
| Check price | 얼마예요? (*eolmayeyo?*) |
| Too spicy | 너무 매워요 (*neomu maewoyo*) |
| Ask for water | 물 좀 주세요 (*mul jom juseyo*) |
| Share the hype | 진짜 맛있어요 (*jinjja masisseoyo*) |

## What does the vendor say back?

Expect quick numbers and nods, not grammar lessons.

You: **이거 하나 주세요.** — *igeo hana juseyo.* — "One of this, please."  
Them: **삼천 원이에요.** — *samcheon wonieyo.* — "It is three thousand won."  
You: **너무 매워요?** — *neomu maewoyo?* — "Is it too spicy?"

If they hand you extra napkins, **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), "Thank you," is enough — no need for a speech.

## What usually goes wrong at night market lines?

Learners study menu vocabulary but freeze on the point-and-order line above, the one every stall owner understands instantly.

A second mistake is eating in silence while friends switch to Korean for two seconds to order — one practiced phrase keeps you in the conversation. For snack runs between stalls, see [Korean phrases at a convenience store](/korean-phrases-convenience-store).
