When Stray Kids dropped **THIS & THAT** on August 6, the Korea Times reported the group had just wrapped five sold-out nights at KSPO Dome — and within hours of the album hitting streaming, Korean fan chats were flooded with short, high-energy phrases they use for every comeback. These lines work whether you are cheering for Stray Kids or any act whose album just dropped.

> - Hype the release: **컴백 대박이에요** (*keombaek daebagieyo*) — "This comeback is incredible"
> - Praise the title track: **타이틀곡 진짜 좋아요** (*taiteulgok jinjja joayo*) — "The title track is so good"
> - Call out a b-side: **수록곡도 너무 좋아요** (*surokgokdo neomu joayo*) — "The b-sides are so good too"
> - Tell a friend to listen: **빨리 들어봐요!** (*ppalli deureobwayo!*) — "Listen to it now!"

## What do Korean fans say the moment an album drops?

The first thing to fire off in a group chat is **컴백 대박이에요** (*keombaek daebagieyo*), literally "The comeback is a jackpot." **대박** (*daebak*) is the all-purpose Korean exclamation for something far beyond expectations — attach it to almost any noun and it sounds natural. **대박이에요** (*daebagieyo*) is the polite form; drop the **이에요** to get the informal **대박이야** (*daebagiya*) for close friends.

After first listen, the chat shifts to track reactions:

| Comeback moment | Fan phrase |
|---|---|
| Album just dropped | 컴백 대박이에요! (*keombaek daebagieyo!*) |
| Title track first impression | 타이틀곡 진짜 좋아요 (*taiteulgok jinjja joayo*) |
| B-side standout | 수록곡도 너무 좋아요 (*surokgokdo neomu joayo*) |
| Urging a friend | 빨리 들어봐요! (*ppalli deureobwayo!*) |

**수록곡** (*surokgok*) means "included tracks" — the non-title songs that often become fan favourites over time. Mentioning one by name shows you went beyond the title track, which Korean fans genuinely respect.

## How do you hype a comeback with a friend in Korean?

The typical exchange runs fast. Keep it short and reactive:

You: **새 앨범 들었어요?** — *sae aelbeom deureosseoyo?* — "Have you heard the new album?"  
Them: **당연하죠! 타이틀곡 너무 좋아요.** — *dangyeonhajyo! taiteulgok neomu joayo.* — "Of course! The title track is so good."  
You: **수록곡도 다 좋더라고요.** — *surokgokdo da jotdeoragoyo.* — "Even the b-sides are all great."

**당연하죠** (*dangyeonhajyo*) means "Of course!" — the fan's way of saying "I was streaming at midnight, obviously." It is one of those phrases that sounds confident and casual at the same time.

## How do you talk about b-sides and standout tracks?

Say **이 곡이 제일 좋아요** (*i gogi jeil joayo*), "This song is my favourite," and name the track. To ask a friend's pick, use **어떤 곡이 제일 좋아요?** (*eotteon gogi jeil joayo?*), "Which song do you like the most?"

If a song grows on you, **들으면 들을수록 좋아져요** (*deureumyeon deureulssurok joajyeoyo*), "The more I listen, the more I like it," says exactly that. It works just as well outside of K-pop — recommending a drama, a restaurant, or a neighbourhood.

## What do you say when you run into another fan in person?

Check with **혹시 팬이세요?** (*hoksi paeniseyo?*), "Are you a fan, by any chance?" — polite and non-intrusive. If yes, **같이 응원해요** (*gachi eungwonhaeyo*), "Let's cheer them on together," is an instant icebreaker that needs no extra small talk.
