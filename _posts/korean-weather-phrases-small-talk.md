Korean weather phrases for small talk open almost every casual chat. Ask **오늘 날씨가 어때요?** (*oneul nalssiga eottaeyo?*), "How is the weather today?", and you give your coworker or host family an easy topic to pick up. Answer **날씨가 좋아요** (*nalssiga joayo*), "The weather is nice," and the conversation starts itself — no hobby, no politics, no pressure.

> - Ask about today: **오늘 날씨가 어때요?** (*oneul nalssiga eottaeyo?*), "How is the weather today?"
> - Say it is nice: **날씨가 좋아요** (*nalssiga joayo*), "The weather is nice."
> - Say it is raining: **비가 와요** (*biga wayo*), "It is raining."
> - Guess from what you see: **비가 올 것 같아요** (*biga ol geot gatayo*), "It looks like it will rain."

## How do you ask about the weather in Korean?

Korea has four sharp seasons, so the weather changes fast and people comment on it constantly. The question pattern is simple: **오늘** (*oneul*, today) + **날씨** (*nalssi*, weather) + **어때요?** (*eottaeyo?*, how is it?).

You: **오늘 날씨가 어때요?** — *oneul nalssiga eottaeyo?* — "How is the weather today?"  
Them: **맑고 따뜻해요.** — *malkgo ttatteuthaeyo.* — "It is clear and warm."  
You: **그래요? 산책하기 좋겠네요.** — *geuraeyo? sanchaekhagi jogetneyo.* — "Really? Good weather for a walk."

If you want a shorter version with a friend, **날씨 어때?** (*nalssi eottae?*) works — drop **-요** when the relationship is casual. With a boss, a host parent, or a stranger, keep **어때요?**.

## What do you say when it rains, snows, or feels humid?

Name what is happening, then add **-요** for polite speech. These cover most days you will actually talk about:

| What you see | Say this |
|---|---|
| Clear sky | 날씨가 맑아요 (*nalssiga malgayo*) |
| Rain | 비가 와요 (*biga wayo*) |
| Snow | 눈이 와요 (*nuni wayo*) |
| Cloudy | 흐려요 (*heuryeo*) |
| Hot and sticky | 덥고 습해요 (*deopgo seuphaeyo*) |
| Cold | 추워요 (*chuwoyo*) |

Summer often means **장마** (*jangma*, monsoon season). Spring and autumn bring **미세먼지** (*mise meonji*, fine dust) — check your weather app before you head out.

## How do Koreans use weather to keep a chat going?

After the first answer, react — do not jump to a new topic. **그래요?** (*geuraeyo?*, "Really?") or **진짜요?** (*jinjayo?*, "For real?") buys you time while you think of the next line.

You: **오늘 날씨가 참 좋아요.** — *oneul nalssiga cham joayo.* — "The weather is really nice today."  
Them: **그쵸? 어제보다 훨씬 따뜻해요.** — *geuchyo? eojeboda hwolssin ttatteuthaeyo.* — "Right? Much warmer than yesterday."  
You: **그러니까요. 밖에 나가고 싶어요.** — *geureonikayo. bakke nagago sipeoyo.* — "Exactly. I want to go outside."

**-나 봐요** (*-na bwayo*) means "I guess" or "it seems." **비가 오나 봐요** (*biga ona bwayo*) is "Looks like rain." You do not need perfect grammar — noticing the sky and saying one honest sentence is enough for small talk.

## Why weather beats "nice to meet you" as a first Korean sentence

Textbooks teach introductions first. Real life in Korea often starts with **오늘 날씨가 어때요?** at the office kitchen, in a taxi, or while waiting for a friend. The phrase is short, polite, and always relevant.
