To ask for directions in a Korean subway, name the exact thing you need and add **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*), "Where is it?" **2호선이 어디예요?** (*ihoseoni eodiyeyo?*) asks "Where is Line 2?" The polite **-요** ending works with any stranger, so one clear question is enough to get moving.

> - Name your destination, then add **어디예요?** — **지하철역이 어디예요?** (*jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?*) asks "Where is the subway station?"
> - Open with **죄송한데요** (*joesonghandeyo*), "Excuse me," before asking a stranger anything.
> - Repeat the key word as a question — **오른쪽이요?** (*oreunjjogiyo?*), "To the right?" — to confirm without a full sentence.
> - If the reply is too fast, say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), "Please say that again."

## Ask for exactly what you need

Korean puts the destination first, then **어디예요?** Swap in whatever you're actually looking for:

| Situation | Ask this |
|---|---|
| Find the station | 지하철역이 어디예요? |
| Find a line | 2호선이 어디예요? |
| Find the platform | 타는 곳이 어디예요? *(taneun gosi eodiyeyo?)* |
| Find a transfer | 갈아타는 곳이 어디예요? *(garataneun gosi eodiyeyo?)* |
| Find an exit | 2번 출구가 어디예요? *(ibeon chulguga eodiyeyo?)* |

Use **지하철역이 어디예요?** outside a station. Once you're inside, ask about the line, platform, or exit instead — a station can have several exits leading to completely different sides of the road.

## Confirm the direction you hear

Open with **죄송한데요** (*joesonghandeyo*), then show the station name on your phone.

You: **죄송한데요. 지하철역이 어디예요?** — *joesonghandeyo. jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?* — "Excuse me. Where is the subway station?"  
Them: **오른쪽으로 가세요.** — *oreunjjogeuro gaseyo.* — "Go to the right."  
You: **오른쪽이요? 감사합니다.** — *oreunjjogiyo? gamsahamnida.* — "To the right? Thank you."

Repeating the direction as a question is the fastest fix I teach: it costs one word and lets the person correct you before you walk off wrong. Never nod and walk away on a word you only half-caught.

## Ask about a line or a transfer

For route questions, name your destination and the decision you need help with:

You: **홍대입구역에 가려면 몇 호선을 타야 해요?** — *Hongdaeipguyeoge garyeomyeon myeot hoseoneul taya haeyo?* — "Which line do I take to get to Hongik University Station?"  
Them: **2호선을 타세요.** — *ihoseoneul taseyo.* — "Take Line 2."

To check a transfer or your stop, ask **여기에서 갈아타요?** (*yeogieseo garatayo?*), "Do I transfer here?" or **이 역에서 내려요?** (*i yeogeseo naeryeoyo?*), "Do I get off at this station?" Both confirm the one fact that changes your route — a plain "next station?" question doesn't.

## Ask for a specific exit

Inside the station, **출구가 어디예요?** (*chulguga eodiyeyo?*) means "Where is the exit?" A numbered version is far more useful: **6번 출구가 어디예요?** (*yukbeon chulguga eodiyeyo?*), "Where is Exit 6?" If you're not sure which one you need, point at the place name on your phone and ask **몇 번 출구로 나가야 해요?** (*myeot beon chulguro nagaya haeyo?*), "Which exit should I use?" Notice **입구** (*ipgu*, entrance) and **출구** (*chulgu*, exit) look similar but are opposites — mixing them up is the most common exit mistake.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I ask where the subway station is in Korean?

Ask **지하철역이 어디예요?** (*jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?*), "Where is the subway station?" Inside a station, swap **지하철역** (*jihacheolyeok*, subway station) for the line, platform, or exit you actually need.

### What should I listen for when someone answers in Korean?

Catch **왼쪽** (*oenjjok*, left), **오른쪽** (*oreunjjok*, right), **곧장** (*gotjang*, straight), and any line or exit number. Repeat that one word with **-이요?** to confirm you heard it right.

### What if I don't understand the Korean reply?

Say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), "Please say that again," or **천천히 말씀해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo*), "Please speak slowly." Then repeat back the one detail you need to confirm.
