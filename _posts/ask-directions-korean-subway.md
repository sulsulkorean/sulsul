In a Korean subway, the most useful question is rarely a general “Where is the subway?” You are usually looking for a particular line, platform, transfer, or numbered exit. Name that destination and add **어디예요?** (*eodiyeyo?*), “Where is it?” For example, **2호선이 어디예요?** (*ihoseoni eodiyeyo?*) asks “Where is Line 2?”

The polite **-요** ending is appropriate with station staff and strangers. You do not need a perfect sentence before asking. A station name on your phone, one clear question, and a confirmation can carry the whole interaction.

> **Quick answer:** Start with **죄송한데요** (*joesonghandeyo*), “Excuse me,” then ask **2호선이 어디예요?** (*ihoseoni eodiyeyo?*), “Where is Line 2?” If the reply is too fast, say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Please say that again.”

## Build the question you actually need

Korean lets you place the destination first and **어디예요?** after it. The small subject particles **이/가** make these questions complete and natural, although people may omit them in quick conversation.

- **지하철역이 어디예요?** — *jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?* — “Where is the subway station?”
- **2호선이 어디예요?** — *ihoseoni eodiyeyo?* — “Where is Line 2?”
- **타는 곳이 어디예요?** — *taneun gosi eodiyeyo?* — “Where is the boarding area?”
- **갈아타는 곳이 어디예요?** — *garataneun gosi eodiyeyo?* — “Where do I transfer?”
- **2번 출구가 어디예요?** — *ibeon chulguga eodiyeyo?* — “Where is Exit 2?”

Use **지하철역이 어디예요?** outside a station when you need to find the station itself. Once you are inside, replace “subway station” with the line, platform, or exit you need. Exit numbers matter because different exits can lead to different sides of a large road.

## Start politely, then show your destination

Open with **죄송한데요** (*joesonghandeyo*). Literally connected to “I’m sorry,” it functions naturally as “Excuse me, but…” when you are about to ask a stranger something. Then show the Korean station name on your map and ask a specific question.

Here is a realistic exchange outside a station:

You: **죄송한데요. 지하철역이 어디예요?** — *joesonghandeyo. jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?* — “Excuse me. Where is the subway station?”  
Them: **저쪽 사거리에서 오른쪽으로 가세요.** — *jeojjok sageorieseo oreunjjogeuro gaseyo.* — “Go right at the intersection over there.”  
You: **사거리에서 오른쪽이요? 감사합니다.** — *sageorieseo oreunjjogiyo? gamsahamnida.* — “Right at the intersection? Thank you.”

Repeating the key direction as a question—**오른쪽이요?** (*oreunjjogiyo?*), “To the right?”—is an easy confirmation technique. It gives the other person a chance to correct you without requiring a long Korean sentence.

Follow these steps when you ask:

1. Move out of the flow of passengers before stopping someone.
2. Say **죄송한데요** to get their attention politely.
3. Show the Korean name of your destination if you have it.
4. Ask one specific question, such as **3호선이 어디예요?**
5. Repeat the line, direction, or exit number you heard.
6. Close with **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*), “Thank you.”

## Understand the direction words in the reply

You do not need to understand every word. Listen for a direction, a landmark, and an action. **-으로/로** marks the direction of movement in phrases such as “to the left” or “this way.”

| What you may hear | Revised Romanization | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **이쪽으로 가세요** | *ijjogeuro gaseyo* | Go this way |
| **저쪽으로 가세요** | *jeojjogeuro gaseyo* | Go that way |
| **곧장 가세요** | *gotjang gaseyo* | Go straight |
| **왼쪽으로 가세요** | *oenjjogeuro gaseyo* | Go to the left |
| **오른쪽으로 가세요** | *oreunjjogeuro gaseyo* | Go to the right |
| **표지판을 따라가세요** | *pyojipaneul ttaragaseyo* | Follow the signs |
| **계단으로 내려가세요** | *gyedaneuro naeryeogaseyo* | Go down the stairs |

**가세요** (*gaseyo*) is a polite instruction meaning “go.” You may also hear **올라가세요** (*ollagaseyo*), “go up,” or **내려가세요** (*naeryeogaseyo*), “go down.” Station staff may point while speaking, so check whether the gesture matches the word you caught.

## Ask about a line, transfer, or stop

For route questions, include both your destination and the decision you need help with. Asking only **몇 호선이에요?** (*myeot hoseonieyo?*), “Which line is it?” can work when the destination is already obvious on your screen. A fuller question is clearer:

You: **홍대입구역에 가려면 몇 호선을 타야 해요?** — *Hongdaeipguyeoge garyeomyeon myeot hoseoneul taya haeyo?* — “Which line should I take to get to Hongik University Station?”  
Them: **2호선을 타세요.** — *ihoseoneul taseyo.* — “Take Line 2.”  
You: **2호선이요? 어디에서 타요?** — *ihoseoniyo? eodieseo tayo?* — “Line 2? Where do I board?”

The pattern **-에 가려면** (*-e garyeomyeon*) means “to get to…” The answer may contain **타세요** (*taseyo*), “take/board,” **갈아타세요** (*garataseyo*), “transfer,” or **내리세요** (*naeriseyo*), “get off.”

When you need to check a transfer, ask **여기에서 갈아타요?** (*yeogieseo garatayo?*), “Do I transfer here?” To check your stop, ask **이 역에서 내려요?** (*i yeogeseo naeryeoyo?*), “Do I get off at this station?” These questions are safer than using **다음 역은 어디예요?**, which only asks what the next station is and does not confirm where you should leave the train.

## Recover when the answer is too fast

Long replies are normal because the person may explain several turns at once. Interrupt politely and reduce the answer to one point:

- **죄송하지만, 다시 말씀해 주세요.** — *joesonghajiman, dasi malsseumhae juseyo.* — “Sorry, but please say that again.”
- **천천히 말씀해 주세요.** — *cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo.* — “Please speak slowly.”
- **여기 맞아요?** — *yeogi majayo?* — “Is this the right place?”
- **이 방향이 맞아요?** — *i banghyangi majayo?* — “Is this the right direction?”

At a transfer corridor, the exchange might sound like this:

You: **죄송하지만, 여기에서 갈아타요?** — *joesonghajiman, yeogieseo garatayo?* — “Sorry, do I transfer here?”  
Them: **아니요. 다음 역에서 갈아타세요.** — *aniyo. daeum yeogeseo garataseyo.* — “No. Transfer at the next station.”  
You: **다음 역이요? 알겠습니다.** — *daeum yeogiyo? algetseumnida.* — “The next station? Got it.”

Do not pretend you understood because someone is waiting. Repeating **다음 역이요?** checks the one detail that changes your route.

## Use the right phrase at exits

Inside the station, **출구가 어디예요?** (*chulguga eodiyeyo?*) means “Where is the exit?” However, a numbered exit question is usually more useful: **6번 출구가 어디예요?** (*yukbeon chulguga eodiyeyo?*), “Where is Exit 6?”

If you are unsure which exit serves your destination, ask **이곳에 가려면 몇 번 출구로 나가야 해요?** (*igose garyeomyeon myeot beon chulguro nagaya haeyo?*), “Which exit should I use to get to this place?” Say it while pointing to the place name on your phone.

You: **이곳에 가려면 몇 번 출구로 나가야 해요?** — *igose garyeomyeon myeot beon chulguro nagaya haeyo?* — “Which exit should I use to get to this place?”  
Them: **4번 출구로 나가세요.** — *sabeon chulguro nagaseyo.* — “Go out through Exit 4.”  
You: **4번 출구요? 고맙습니다.** — *sabeon chulguyo? gomapseumnida.* — “Exit 4? Thank you.”

Notice the difference between **입구** (*ipgu*), “entrance,” and **출구** (*chulgu*), “exit.” Also distinguish **역** (*yeok*), “station,” from **호선** (*hoseon*), “subway line.” These four nouns prevent many vague questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I ask where the subway station is in Korean?

Ask **지하철역이 어디예요?** (*jihacheolyeogi eodiyeyo?*), “Where is the subway station?” Inside a station, replace **지하철역** with the line or exit you need.

### What should I listen for when asking for directions in Korean?

Catch the essential words: **왼쪽** (*oenjjok*), “left”; **오른쪽** (*oreunjjok*), “right”; **곧장** (*gotjang*), “straight”; and a line or exit number. You can confirm by repeating that word with **-이요?**

### How do I say “Where is Exit 2?” in Korean?

Say **2번 출구가 어디예요?** (*ibeon chulguga eodiyeyo?*). If you do not know the exit number, show your destination and ask which exit you should use.

### What if I don't understand the Korean reply?

Say **다시 말씀해 주세요** (*dasi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Please say that again,” or **천천히 말씀해 주세요** (*cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo*), “Please speak slowly.” Then repeat the one detail you need to confirm.
