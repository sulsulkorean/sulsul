Korean phrases for the pharmacy start with knowing what you need. Say **두통약 있어요?** (*dutong yak isseoyo?*), "Do you have headache medicine?", and you get what you came for in ten seconds. Say **이 약 어떻게 먹어요?** (*i yak eotteoke meogeoyo?*), "How do I take this medicine?", and you leave knowing the dosage. Every other pharmacy visit is a variation on those two questions.

> - Ask for headache medicine: **두통약 있어요?** (*dutong yak isseoyo?*), "Do you have headache medicine?"
> - Ask for antacid: **소화제 있어요?** (*sohwaje isseoyo?*), "Do you have antacid?"
> - Ask about dosage: **이 약 어떻게 먹어요?** (*i yak eotteoke meogeoyo?*), "How do I take this medicine?"
> - Report an allergy: **알레르기 있어요** (*allereugi isseoyo*), "I have an allergy."

## How do you ask for medicine at a Korean pharmacy?

Korean pharmacies (약국, *yakguk*) are easy to find — look for the green cross sign. They carry fever reducers, pain relievers, antacids, cold medicine, and allergy pills without a prescription. The pharmacist speaks enough to help if you name what you need.

Name the symptom and add **있어요?** (*isseoyo?*) — "Do you have…?" — and the pharmacist will grab the right box.

| Symptom | Say this |
|---|---|
| Headache | 두통약 있어요? (*dutong yak isseoyo?*) |
| Stomach pain | 소화제 있어요? (*sohwaje isseoyo?*) |
| Fever | 해열제 있어요? (*haeyeolje isseoyo?*) |
| Cold or runny nose | 감기약 있어요? (*gamgiyak isseoyo?*) |

If you want to say where it hurts, point and say **여기 아파요** (*yeogi apayo*), "It hurts here." Pointing is allowed. Pharmacists see it every day.

## What do you say when you have an allergy or need a prescription?

Tell the pharmacist before they hand you anything:

**알레르기 있어요** — *allereugi isseoyo* — "I have an allergy."

Follow it with the allergen if you know it in Korean. Penicillin is **페니실린** (*penisyllin*), aspirin is **아스피린** (*aseupirin*), and ibuprofen is **이부프로펜** (*ibupeurofen*). For anything complicated, show the pharmacist the name on your phone in English — they can read it.

If your medicine requires a prescription (**처방전**, *cheobangjeon*), the pharmacist will tell you right away. Say **처방전 없이 살 수 있어요?** (*cheobangjeon eopsi sal su isseoyo?*), "Can I buy it without a prescription?" to check before you look for a clinic.

## What happens when the pharmacist speaks and you don't follow?

Most dosage instructions follow the same pattern. Listen for three things: the number, the time word, and 드세요 (*deuseyo*), "please take."

You: **이 약 어떻게 먹어요?** — *i yak eotteoke meogeoyo?* — "How do I take this medicine?"  
Them: **하루에 세 번, 식후에 드세요.** — *harue se beon, sikhue deuseyo.* — "Three times a day, after meals."  
You: **알겠어요. 감사합니다.** — *algessoyo. gamsahamnida.* — "I understand. Thank you."

**하루에** (*harue*) means "per day." **식후** (*sikhu*) means "after eating." **식전** (*sikjeon*) means "before eating." Those four words decode every standard dosage instruction a Korean pharmacist will give you.
