If your wallet disappears in Korea, begin with **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** (*jigabeul ireobeoryeosseoyo*): “I lost my wallet.” The next useful details are where you last had it, when that was, what it looks like, and how someone can contact you.

Do not turn one missing item into a long emergency speech. First check your pockets and bag, retrace the last few minutes, and ask staff at the place where you last used the wallet. At the same time, follow your card issuers’ instructions to lock or report payment cards. A lost wallet may require several separate actions; no single Korean sentence or office can promise its return.

> **Quick answer:** Say **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** (*jigabeul ireobeoryeosseoyo*), “I lost my wallet,” followed by **여기에서 마지막으로 봤어요** (*yeogieseo majimageuro bwasseoyo*), “I last saw it here.” Then show a photo or describe the wallet.

## Say what happened without overexplaining

The verb **잃어버리다** (*ireobeorida*) means “to lose” something. In the past polite form, it becomes **잃어버렸어요** (*ireobeoryeosseoyo*), “lost.” Put the missing object before it:

- **지갑을 잃어버렸어요.** — *jigabeul ireobeoryeosseoyo.* — “I lost my wallet.”
- **카드를 잃어버렸어요.** — *kadeureul ireobeoryeosseoyo.* — “I lost my card.”
- **지갑을 찾고 있어요.** — *jigabeul chatgo isseoyo.* — “I’m looking for my wallet.”
- **도와주세요.** — *dowajuseyo.* — “Please help me.”

Use **도와주세요** when you need active help, but say what the problem is first. “I lost my wallet. Please help me” gives a station employee or shop worker something concrete to respond to.

If you are not sure whether the wallet is lost or merely left nearby, **지갑이 안 보여요** (*jigabi an boyeoyo*), “I can’t see/find my wallet,” is also natural. It sounds less final while you are still searching.

## Retrace the moment it went missing

Your best first conversation is often with staff at the last shop, café, taxi, hotel, or station where you remember using the wallet. Ask whether anyone turned one in; do not claim that it was stolen unless you have a reason to believe that.

You: **죄송한데요. 지갑을 잃어버렸어요.** — *joesonghandeyo. jigabeul ireobeoryeosseoyo.* — “Excuse me. I lost my wallet.”  
Them: **마지막으로 어디에서 봤어요?** — *majimageuro eodieseo bwasseoyo?* — “Where did you last see it?”  
You: **계산할 때 여기에서 봤어요.** — *gyesanhal ttae yeogieseo bwasseoyo.* — “I saw it here when I paid.”

Useful time and place phrases include:

- **한 시간 전쯤이었어요.** — *han sigan jeonjjumieosseoyo.* — “It was about an hour ago.”
- **이 자리에서 사용했어요.** — *i jarieseo sayonghaesseoyo.* — “I used it at this seat/place.”
- **혹시 들어온 지갑이 있나요?** — *hoksi deureoon jigabi innayo?* — “Has a wallet possibly been turned in?”

The last question does not assume that staff have your property. **혹시** (*hoksi*) softens it with the sense of “by any chance.”

## Take practical steps in a sensible order

The right channel depends on where the wallet disappeared and whether there is an immediate safety issue. Staff at a specific venue or transport operator may be able to check their own lost-property process. Police channels may be appropriate for a loss report or suspected crime, but procedures and available language support can vary.

1. Check every compartment of your clothes and bags, then return to the last place you used the wallet if it is safe to do so.
2. Ask staff **혹시 들어온 지갑이 있나요?** while showing a clear description or photo.
3. Contact each payment-card issuer through its official app or phone number and follow its instructions for locking or reporting the card.
4. Record the approximate time, location, wallet description, and cards or identification that were inside.
5. Ask the relevant venue, transport operator, lost-property service, or police contact what reporting option applies to your case.
6. If you see unauthorized activity or believe a crime is occurring, explain that specific issue rather than reporting only a missing object.

Korea’s police number is **112**, but a routine lost-property search and an urgent police situation are not the same. Use emergency assistance for an urgent police need. For a nonurgent loss, start with the place or service connected to where the item went missing and follow official instructions given for that case.

## Describe the wallet so it can be identified

Color alone may not distinguish your wallet. Combine its material, shape, brand if visible, and contents—but avoid announcing sensitive card numbers or identification numbers in public.

| Detail | Korean phrase | Revised Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Color | **검은색 지갑이에요** | *geomeunsaek jigabieyo* | It is a black wallet |
| Material | **가죽 지갑이에요** | *gajuk jigabieyo* | It is a leather wallet |
| Shape | **작은 반지갑이에요** | *jageun banjigabieyo* | It is a small folding wallet |
| Contents | **안에 카드가 들어 있어요** | *ane kadeuga deureo isseoyo* | There are cards inside |
| Distinctive feature | **앞에 작은 로고가 있어요** | *ape jageun rogoga isseoyo* | There is a small logo on the front |
| Contact | **이 번호로 연락해 주세요** | *i beonhoro yeollakhae juseyo* | Please contact me at this number |

In **검은색 지갑이에요**, Korean normally places the description before the noun: “black wallet.” **반지갑** (*banjigap*) refers to a wallet that folds, while **장지갑** (*jangjigap*) is a long wallet.

A staff member may ask **어떻게 생겼어요?** (*eotteoke saenggyeosseoyo?*), “What does it look like?” You can answer with two or three identifying details: **검은색 가죽 반지갑이에요** (*geomeunsaek gajuk banjigabieyo*), “It’s a black leather folding wallet.”

## Handle a report conversation accurately

At a police facility or another lost-property desk, the person may need facts rather than a polished story. Give the last confirmed location and time. If you only noticed the loss on the subway, do not automatically say you lost it there; say where you noticed it.

You: **지갑 분실 신고를 하고 싶어요.** — *jigap bunsil singoreul hago sipeoyo.* — “I’d like to report a lost wallet.”  
Them: **언제 없어진 걸 알았어요?** — *eonje eopseojin geol arasseoyo?* — “When did you realize it was missing?”  
You: **지하철에서 내린 후에 알았어요.** — *jihacheoreseo naerin hue arasseoyo.* — “I realized after I got off the subway.”

That answer is more precise than **지하철에서 잃어버렸어요**, “I lost it on the subway,” if you do not actually know where it was lost.

You may also hear **신분증이 들어 있었어요?** (*sinbunjeungi deureo isseosseoyo?*), “Was there identification inside?” or **연락 가능한 번호가 있어요?** (*yeollak ganeunghan beonhoga isseoyo?*), “Is there a number where you can be reached?” Answer only what you know.

## Ask about an item without assuming it was found

The original question **제 지갑을 찾았어요?** can sound like “Did you look for/find my wallet?” A clearer status question is **제 지갑이 들어왔나요?** (*je jigabi deureowannayo?*), “Has my wallet been turned in?” Give context so the person can identify the correct report.

You: **안녕하세요. 제 지갑이 들어왔나요?** — *annyeonghaseyo. je jigabi deureowannayo?* — “Hello. Has my wallet been turned in?”  
Them: **접수 번호가 있나요?** — *jeopsu beonhoga innayo?* — “Do you have a report/reference number?”  
You: **네, 여기 있어요.** — *ne, yeogi isseoyo.* — “Yes, here it is.”

If someone returns the wallet directly, say **지갑을 찾아 주셔서 감사합니다** (*jigabeul chaja jusyeoseo gamsahamnida*), “Thank you for finding my wallet.” Before leaving, check the item calmly and follow any identification process requested by the office holding it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I report a lost wallet in Korea?

Say **지갑을 잃어버렸어요** (*jigabeul ireobeoryeosseoyo*), “I lost my wallet.” Then give the last confirmed time and place. If making a formal report, **지갑 분실 신고를 하고 싶어요** means “I’d like to report a lost wallet.”

### What should I do if I can't find my wallet in Korea?

Retrace your route, ask the relevant venue or transport staff, secure payment cards through their issuers, and record identifying details. The appropriate reporting route depends on where and how the wallet disappeared.

### How do I ask for help in Korean?

Say **도와주세요** (*dowajuseyo*), “Please help me,” after stating the problem. For example: **지갑을 잃어버렸어요. 도와주세요.**

### What is the emergency number in Korea?

**112** connects callers to police. It is relevant to an urgent police situation; a nonurgent lost-property inquiry may be better directed first to the place, operator, or official lost-property channel connected with the loss.
