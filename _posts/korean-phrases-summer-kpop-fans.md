Red Velvet released **Velvet Summer** on August 7, k-popped.com reported — and Korean fan group chats immediately shifted from streaming links to weekend plans. Summer in Korea means heat complaints, bingsu runs, and Han River evenings, and the Korean for all of it is short, casual, and easy to drop into any conversation.

> - Complain about heat: **오늘 너무 더워요** (*oneul neomu deowoyo*) — "It's so hot today"
> - Suggest the beach: **바다 가고 싶어요** (*bada gago sipeoyo*) — "I want to go to the beach"
> - Propose bingsu: **빙수 먹으러 가요** (*bingsu meogeureo gayo*) — "Let's go get bingsu"
> - React with excitement: **완전 신나요!** (*wanjeon sinnayo!*) — "I'm so excited!"

## How do Koreans talk about summer heat without sounding stiff?

**오늘 너무 더워요** (*oneul neomu deowoyo*), "It's so hot today," opens almost any August conversation. **너무** (*neomu*) means "so" or "too much" — stack it onto any feeling for natural emphasis. For extreme heat, Koreans add **죽겠어요** (*jukgesseoyo*), "I'm going to die," which sounds dramatic but is completely normal small talk at 35°C.

Summer heat phrases to have ready:

| Situation | Say this |
|---|---|
| It's extremely hot | 너무 더워서 죽겠어요 (*neomu deowooseo jukgesseoyo*) |
| It's humid too | 습해서 더 힘들어요 (*seupaeseo deo himeureoyo*) |
| It's finally cooler | 오늘은 좀 시원해요 (*oneureum jom siwonhaeyo*) |
| Suggest air-conditioning | 에어컨 있는 데 가요 (*eeokon inneun de gayo*) |

**습해요** (*seuphaeyo*), "It's humid," separates learners from people who actually survive Korean summers. Seoul in August is sticky as well as hot — mentioning the humidity lands more naturally than **더워요** (*deowoyo*) alone.

## What do you say when planning a summer outing in Korean?

Start with **이번 주말에 뭐 해요?** (*ibeon jumale mwo haeyo?*), "What are you doing this weekend?" — the go-to opener for making plans in Korean.

You: **이번 주말에 바다 가고 싶어요.** — *ibeon jumale bada gago sipeoyo.* — "I want to go to the beach this weekend."  
Them: **진짜요? 저도요! 같이 가요.** — *jinjjayo? jeodoyo! gachi gayo.* — "Really? Me too! Let's go together."  
You: **좋아요, 아침 일찍 출발해요.** — *joayo, achim iljjik chulbalhaeyo.* — "Great, let's leave early in the morning."

**진짜요?** (*jinjjayo?*), "Really?", is an excited reaction that works in any context. Pair it with **저도요** (*jeodoyo*), "Me too," for a natural two-word agreement.

## How do you talk about Korean summer foods and Han River evenings?

**빙수** (*bingsu*, Korean shaved ice) is the season's defining dessert. Suggest it with **빙수 먹으러 가요** (*bingsu meogeureo gayo*), "Let's go get bingsu." For Han River evenings, **오늘 한강 갈래요?** (*oneul hangang galliaeyo?*), "Want to go to the Han River today?", is one of the most-texted lines in Seoul from June through August.

React to any summer highlight — a view, a bingsu, a concert, a sunset — with **완전 신나요!** (*wanjeon sinnayo!*), "I'm so excited!" **완전** (*wanjeon*) is the intensifier younger Koreans prefer over the more formal **정말** (*jeongmal*) and it fits summer chat perfectly.
