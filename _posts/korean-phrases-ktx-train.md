The KTX (*케이티엑스*, *ke-i-ti-ekseu*) connects Seoul to Busan in about two and a half hours and to most other major Korean cities just as fast. Tickets are sold at staffed counters and self-service kiosks — and knowing a few Korean phrases at the counter means you get exactly the seat and departure you want without guessing at buttons. The single most useful phrase is **부산 가는 표 한 장 주세요** (*Busan ganeun pyo han jang juseyo*), "One ticket to Busan, please."

> - Buy a ticket: **부산 가는 표 한 장 주세요** (*Busan ganeun pyo han jang juseyo*), "One ticket to Busan, please."
> - Find your platform: **몇 번 홈이에요?** (*myeot beon homi-eyo?*), "Which platform number?"
> - Confirm your seat: **제 자리가 맞아요?** (*je jariga majayo?*), "Is this my seat?"
> - Check the next stop: **다음 역이 어디예요?** (*daeum yeok-i eodiyeyo?*), "What is the next station?"

## How do you buy a KTX ticket at the counter?

Go to the staffed window (유인 창구, *yuin changgu*) if you prefer a person to the kiosk. State your destination first, then wait for the follow-up question about timing.

You: **부산 가는 표 한 장 주세요.** — *Busan ganeun pyo han jang juseyo.* — "One ticket to Busan, please."  
Them: **몇 시 열차로 드릴까요?** — *myeot si yeolcharo deurilkkayo?* — "Which departure time would you like?"  
You: **가장 빠른 걸로 주세요.** — *gajang ppareun geollo juseyo.* — "The earliest one, please."

The pattern works for any city — swap "Busan" for your destination: **대구 가는 표 한 장 주세요** (*Daegu ganeun pyo han jang juseyo*), **경주 가는 표 한 장 주세요** (*Gyeongju ganeun pyo han jang juseyo*), and so on.

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| One ticket to Busan | **부산 가는 표 한 장 주세요** (*Busan ganeun pyo han jang juseyo*) |
| Two tickets | **두 장 주세요** (*du jang juseyo*) |
| Earliest departure | **가장 빠른 걸로 주세요** (*gajang ppareun geollo juseyo*) |
| Window seat | **창가 자리로 주세요** (*changga jariro juseyo*) |

## How do you find the right platform?

After buying your ticket, check the departure board — **출발** (*chulbal*) means "departure." Your ticket shows the platform number (홈, *hom*) and car number (호, *ho*). If the platform is not clear, ask at the information desk:

**몇 번 홈이에요?** (*myeot beon homi-eyo?*) — "Which platform number is it?"

Once on the platform, look at the edge markers — they show which car stops where. Match the car number on your ticket to the number on the platform. If you are confused, point to your ticket and ask any staff member — **이 자리가 어디예요?** (*i jariga eodiyeyo?*), "Where is this seat?"

## What do you do if someone is in your seat?

Seat mix-ups happen on busy routes. Do not silently stand in the aisle — just say **죄송한데요, 제 자리인 것 같아요** (*joesonghande-yo, je jari-in geot gatayo*), "Sorry, I think this might be my seat." Show your ticket. The other person will check their own ticket and move if there is a mistake. No raised voices needed.

If a large bag needs to go to the overhead rack and you cannot reach, ask the nearest staff member: **짐 올리는 것 좀 도와주실 수 있어요?** (*jim ollineun geot jom dowa-jusil su isseoyo?*), "Could you help me lift this luggage?"

## How do you know when you are approaching your stop?

Announcements play in Korean and English on most KTX routes, so you will usually hear your station name. The screen inside the car also shows the next stop. To double-check with the person next to you, ask **다음 역이 어디예요?** (*daeum yeok-i eodiyeyo?*), "What is the next station?"

When the announcement comes for your stop, start gathering your things a few minutes early. The exit is at both ends of the car — follow the **출구** (*chulgu*, "exit") signs.
