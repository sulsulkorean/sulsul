KCON 2026, the world's biggest K-pop festival, takes over LA next week — and the phrases fans actually shout still work in Korean even if you are not at the stadium yet. **화이팅!** (*hwaiting!*) cheers someone on, and **공연 정말 기대돼요** (*gongyeon jeongmal gidaedwaeyo*), "I am really looking forward to the show," is what you text a friend before doors open.

> - Cheer your artist: **화이팅!** (*hwaiting!*), "You can do it!"
> - Share excitement: **공연 정말 기대돼요** (*gongyeon jeongmal gidaedwaeyo*), "I am really looking forward to the show."
> - Ask about merch: **굿즈 어디서 사요?** (*gutjeu eodiseo sayo?*), "Where do I buy merch?"
> - Thank staff: **정말 감사합니다** (*jeongmal gamsahamnida*), "Thank you so much."

## How do you use Korean before you even land in LA?

Fan communities mix English and Korean, but a few Korean lines signal you are part of the room — not just watching from the sidelines.

1. Text your group chat: **KCON 가요?** (*KCON gayo?*), "Are you going to KCON?"
2. Reply with **공연 정말 기대돼요** when someone shares their ticket.
3. At the venue line, yell **화이팅!** (*hwaiting!*) when an artist banner rolls by.
4. Ask staff **굿즈 어디서 사요?** if the merch map is all in Korean.
5. After a set, tell a friend **최고였어요** (*choegoyeosseoyo*), "It was the best."

| Moment | Say this |
|---|---|
| Hype before doors | 공연 정말 기대돼요 (*gongyeon jeongmal gidaedwaeyo*) |
| Cheer passersby | 화이팅! (*hwaiting!*) |
| Find merch | 굿즈 어디서 사요? (*gutjeu eodiseo sayo?*) |
| After a great set | 최고였어요 (*choegoyeosseoyo*) |
| Polite thanks | 정말 감사합니다 (*jeongmal gamsahamnida*) |

## What do Korean speakers say back?

Replies at fan events stay loud and short.

You: **오늘 공연 와요?** — *oneul gongyeon wayo?* — "Are you coming to the show today?"  
Them: **네, 벌써 기대돼요!** — *ne, beolsseo gidaedwaeyo!* — "Yes, I am already excited!"  
You: **화이팅!** — *hwaiting!*

If someone hands you a freebie, **고마워요** (*gomawoyo*) works with peers; **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) is safer with staff. Match the person, not the grammar chart.

## What usually goes wrong for first-time KCON fans?

Learners memorize **사랑해요** (*saranghaeyo*) from dramas but never practice **화이팅!** (*hwaiting!*), which is what crowds actually yell between songs. Say it out loud once before you need it.

A second mistake is shouting English-only cheers in a line where everyone else switches to Korean for two seconds — you do not need fluency, just one phrase delivered clearly. For more concert-specific lines, see [Korean fan phrases at a K-pop concert](/korean-phrases-kpop-concert).
