Korean phrases for bargaining in markets start with **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*), "How much is this?" A lower price is only worth asking when a seller signals flexibility — most Korean shops, market stalls included, run on fixed prices.

> - Ask **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*), "How much is this?" before assuming a price is negotiable.
> - Check with **정찰제예요?** (*jeongchaljeyeyo?*), "Is it fixed-price?" If yes, the number on the tag is final.
> - Where flexibility is signaled, ask once: **조금 깎아 주실 수 있어요?** (*jogeum kkakka jusil su isseoyo?*), "Could you lower the price a little?"
> - Accept the answer with **알겠습니다** (*algetseumnida*), "I understand."

## Check the Price Before You Ask for Less

**이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*) gets you the number; **정찰제예요?** (*jeongchaljeyeyo?*) tells you if it can move at all. **정찰제** (*jeongchalje*) is a marked fixed-price system, common at department stores and many market stalls.

You: **이거 얼마예요?** — *igeo eolmayeyo?* — "How much is this?"  
Them: **만 오천 원이에요. 정찰제예요.** — *man ocheon wonieyo. jeongchaljeyeyo.* — "It's 15,000 won. It's fixed-price."  
You: **알겠습니다.** — *algetseumnida.* — "I understand."

What usually goes wrong: learners keep pushing after hearing **정찰제예요** (*jeongchaljeyeyo*). That answer ends it — accepting it politely is correct, not weaker.

## Ask Once, Only Where the Seller Signals Room

If a seller mentions the price can move, ask: **조금 깎아 주실 수 있어요?** (*jogeum kkakka jusil su isseoyo?*), "Could you lower the price a little?" Avoid opening with **너무 비싸요** (*neomu bissayo*), "It's too expensive" — **조금 비싸네요** (*jogeum bissaneyo*), "That's a little expensive," states your reaction without demanding change.

You: **조금 비싸네요. 깎아 주실 수 있어요?** — *jogeum bissaneyo. kkakka jusil su isseoyo?* — "That's a little expensive. Could you lower it?"  
Them: **조금은 가능해요.** — *jogeumeun ganeunghaeyo.* — "A little is possible."  
You: **감사합니다. 이걸로 주세요.** — *gamsahamnida. igeollo juseyo.* — "Thank you. I'll take this one."

## Ask About a Bundle Instead of Assuming One

When you want two items and the seller shows flexibility, ask about the total rather than naming a number yourself.

| Ask this | It means |
|---|---|
| 두 개 사면 얼마예요? *(du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?)* | How much if I buy two? |
| 최종 가격이 얼마예요? *(choejong gagyeogi eolmayeyo?)* | What is the final price? |
| 카드 돼요? *(kadeu dwaeyo?)* | Can I pay by card? |

These ask rather than assume a bundle discount exists.

## Close the Conversation Either Way

You: **두 개 사면 얼마예요?** — *du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?* — "How much if I buy two?"  
Them: **두 개에 이만 오천 원이에요.** — *du gaee iman ocheon wonieyo.* — "It's 25,000 won for two."  
You: **좋아요, 두 개 주세요.** — *joayo, du gae juseyo.* — "Great, I'll take two."

If the price doesn't work, close with **생각해 볼게요** (*saenggakhae bolgeyo*), "I'll think about it," rather than treating a refusal as a contest.

## A Four-Step Sequence for Any Stall

1. Greet the seller with **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*), "Hello."
2. Ask the price with **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*).
3. Ask once, only if the seller signals room: **깎아 주실 수 있어요?**
4. Accept the answer with **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) or **생각해 볼게요**.

A note from Yona: the useful skill isn't a lower number — it's asking once and recognizing a fixed-price answer.
