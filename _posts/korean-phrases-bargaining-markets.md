Useful Korean phrases for bargaining begin with one important question: is negotiation actually welcome here? A traditional-looking market is not automatically a bargaining zone, and a small shop does not automatically have flexible prices. Posted prices in department stores, convenience stores, supermarkets, chain shops, and most modern retailers should be treated as fixed. Many market stalls also use fixed prices.

A polite price request makes sense only when the seller explicitly suggests flexibility, a sign advertises a negotiable or bundle offer, an item has no posted price, or you are discussing several items and the seller invites a combined-price conversation. Even then, the seller can simply say no. The Korean below helps you ask once, understand the answer, and move on gracefully.

## First Check Whether the Price Is Fixed

Begin with information, not pressure. These phrases establish the price and whether a listed promotion applies:

- **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*) — “How much is this?”
- **가격표에 있는 금액이 맞아요?** (*gagyeokpyoe inneun geumaegi majayo?*) — “Is the amount on the price tag correct?”
- **이거 할인 상품이에요?** (*igeo harin sangpumieyo?*) — “Is this a discounted item?”
- **정찰제예요?** (*jeongchaljeyeyo?*) — “Is it fixed-price?”

**정찰제** (*jeongchalje*) refers to a system of clearly marked fixed prices. If the answer is yes, do not continue bargaining. A promotion sign may already show the final price, so read it before asking for another reduction.

You: **이거 얼마예요?** — *igeo eolmayeyo?* — “How much is this?”  
Them: **이만 원이에요. 정찰제예요.** — *iman wonieyo. jeongchaljeyeyo.* — “It’s 20,000 won. It’s fixed-price.”  
You: **알겠습니다.** — *algetseumnida.* — “I understand.”

That final response is enough. Politeness here means accepting the answer, not finding a stronger phrase.

## Ask Once When Negotiation Is Clearly Welcome

If the seller says the price can be discussed, use a question rather than a command:

**조금 깎아 주실 수 있어요?** — *jogeum kkakka jusil su isseoyo?* — “Could you lower the price a little?”

The verb **깎다** (*kkakda*) means “to reduce” or “to cut,” including reducing a price. Another neutral option is:

**할인 가능해요?** — *harin ganeunghaeyo?* — “Is a discount possible?”

Neither sentence guarantees a discount. They only ask whether one is available. Avoid opening with **너무 비싸요** (*neomu bissayo*), “It’s too expensive,” because it can sound like a judgment about the seller’s price. The softer **조금 비싸네요** (*jogeum bissaneyo*), “That is a little expensive,” presents your reaction without demanding a change.

You: **조금 비싸네요. 할인 가능해요?** — *jogeum bissaneyo. harin ganeunghaeyo?* — “That is a little expensive. Is a discount possible?”  
Them: **조금은 가능해요.** — *jogeumeun ganeunghaeyo.* — “A small discount is possible.”  
You: **그럼 최종 가격이 얼마예요?** — *geureom choejong gagyeogi eolmayeyo?* — “Then what is the final price?”

Ask the final price instead of inventing a percentage or naming a dramatic reduction. Let the seller state what they can offer.

## Ask About a Bundle, Not an Assumed Discount

When you genuinely want multiple items and the seller has indicated flexibility, ask about the combined total:

- **두 개 사면 얼마예요?** (*du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?*) — “How much is it if I buy two?”
- **세 개 같이 사면 할인돼요?** (*se gae gachi samyeon harindwaeyo?*) — “Is there a discount if I buy three together?”
- **이것까지 같이 하면 얼마예요?** (*igeotkkaji gachi hamyeon eolmayeyo?*) — “How much is it with this included too?”

These are questions, not claims that bulk discounts are standard. A seller may quote the same per-item price, offer a total, or explain an existing promotion.

You: **두 개 사면 얼마예요?** — *du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?* — “How much is it if I buy two?”  
Them: **두 개에 삼만 원이에요.** — *du gaee samman wonieyo.* — “It’s 30,000 won for two.”  
You: **네, 두 개 주세요.** — *ne, du gae juseyo.* — “Yes, I’ll take two.”

Do not assume cash creates a special discount. If a seller independently mentions different payment terms, confirm exactly what they said before agreeing. Otherwise, simply ask **카드 돼요?** (*kadeu dwaeyo?*), “Can I pay by card?” or state **카드로 할게요** (*kadeuro halgeyo*), “I’ll pay by card.”

## Understand the Seller’s Answer

A successful interaction is not the same thing as receiving a lower price. Success means understanding the terms and responding appropriately.

Common answers include:

- **할인 안 돼요** (*harin an dwaeyo*) — “A discount is not possible.”
- **이게 최저 가격이에요** (*ige choejeo gagyeogieyo*) — “This is the lowest price.”
- **여기 적힌 가격이에요** (*yeogi jeokhin gagyeogieyo*) — “It is the price written here.”
- **조금 깎아 드릴게요** (*jogeum kkakka deurilgeyo*) — “I’ll lower it a little for you.”
- **두 개 사시면 할인돼요** (*du gae sasimyeon harindwaeyo*) — “There is a discount if you buy two.”

Notice **드릴게요** (*deurilgeyo*) in a seller’s offer. It politely signals what the seller will do for you. Wait for a clear total before paying:

You: **최종 가격이 얼마예요?** — *choejong gagyeogi eolmayeyo?* — “What is the final price?”  
Them: **만 팔천 원이에요.** — *man palcheon wonieyo.* — “It’s 18,000 won.”  
You: **좋아요. 이걸로 주세요.** — *joayo. igeollo juseyo.* — “Great. I’ll take this one.”

## Decline Without Turning It Into a Contest

If the price does not work for you, close the conversation cleanly:

- **생각해 볼게요** (*saenggakhae bolgeyo*) — “I’ll think about it.”
- **조금 더 둘러볼게요** (*jogeum deo dulleobolgeyo*) — “I’ll look around a little more.”
- **오늘은 안 살게요** (*oneureun an salgeyo*) — “I won’t buy it today.”
- **감사합니다** (*gamsahamnida*) — “Thank you.”

Walking away is not a bargaining tactic that guarantees the seller will call you back. Use these phrases only when you genuinely want to leave.

## A Respectful Six-Step Shopping Sequence

1. Greet the seller with **안녕하세요** (*annyeonghaseyo*), “Hello.”
2. Check the price with **이거 얼마예요?** (*igeo eolmayeyo?*).
3. Look for a price tag or promotion sign.
4. If the seller permits discussion, ask once: **할인 가능해요?** (*harin ganeunghaeyo?*).
5. Confirm the result: **최종 가격이 얼마예요?** (*choejong gagyeogi eolmayeyo?*).
6. Buy with **이걸로 주세요** (*igeollo juseyo*), “I’ll take this,” or leave with **생각해 볼게요** (*saenggakhae bolgeyo*).

| Ask the price | 이거 얼마예요? | *igeo eolmayeyo?* | How much is this? |
| Check fixed pricing | 정찰제예요? | *jeongchaljeyeyo?* | Is it fixed-price? |
| Ask about a promotion | 이거 할인 상품이에요? | *igeo harin sangpumieyo?* | Is this a discounted item? |
| Ask, when permitted | 할인 가능해요? | *harin ganeunghaeyo?* | Is a discount possible? |
| Ask for two-item total | 두 개 사면 얼마예요? | *du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?* | How much if I buy two? |

| Decline | 생각해 볼게요 | *saenggakhae bolgeyo* | I’ll think about it |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you politely ask for a discount in Korean?

When the seller has made it clear that negotiation is possible, ask **조금 깎아 주실 수 있어요?** (*jogeum kkakka jusil su isseoyo?*), “Could you lower the price a little?” Ask once and accept the answer.

### Can you bargain everywhere in Korea?

No. Treat displayed prices as fixed unless the seller explicitly permits negotiation or offers a bundle. Department stores, supermarkets, convenience stores, chain retailers, and many independent shops use fixed prices; market location alone does not tell you the policy.

### How do you say “That is a little expensive” in Korean?

Say **조금 비싸네요** (*jogeum bissaneyo*), “That is a little expensive.” It is softer than **너무 비싸요** (*neomu bissayo*), “It is too expensive,” but it still does not obligate the seller to lower the price.

### What should you say if the seller refuses?

Say **알겠습니다** (*algetseumnida*), “I understand.” Then either pay the stated price or say **생각해 볼게요** (*saenggakhae bolgeyo*), “I’ll think about it.”

## Practice the Question—and the “No”

The most useful rehearsal includes both outcomes. Practice asking once, hearing that the price is fixed, and ending politely. That prepares you for a real interaction without turning every purchase into a negotiation.
